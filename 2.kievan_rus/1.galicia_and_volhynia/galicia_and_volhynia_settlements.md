# Gazetteer Overview and Methodology (10000BC - 1300AD)

This companion document details the settlement-level gazetteer
(comprising 185 distinct archaeological sites and pre-Mongol and
devolved-principality settlements) for Galicia and Volhynia in
South-Western Rus (10000BC - 1300AD). All benchmark series are strictly
book-ended at the Mongol Conquests (1240-1241AD campaign of Batu Khan
across Volhynia and Galicia) and the immediate post-conquest Romanovichi
Kingdom of Galicia-Volhynia (Kingdom of Rus, 1250AD - 1300AD)
[\[1\]](#ref-krypyakevych1984galicia)[\[2\]](#ref-kotlyar1998galician)[\[3\]](#ref-vojtovych2011borders).

## Archaeological Planimetry and Density Multipliers

Population estimates for individual settlements in the regional
gazetteer are derived from three primary archaeological and chronicle
methodologies spanning prehistoric planimetry, medieval rampart
enclosures, and post-Mongol stratigraphic horizons.

For the prehistoric, Eneolithic, and Early Iron Age horizons (10000BC -
300AD), Upper Palaeolithic and Neolithic estimates (10000BC - 5000BC)
are derived from multilayered Dniester canyon and Tisza periglacial camp
horizons at Molodova I and V, Korman IV, Babin I, Voronovitsa, Korolevo,
and Kulychivka (110 - 220 inhabitants) and Bug-Dniester and Linear
Pottery (*LBK*) agricultural villages at Soroky, Bazkiv Ostriv, and
Nezvysko (280 - 420 inhabitants)
[\[4\]](#ref-chernysh1987molodova)[\[5\]](#ref-videiko2004trypillia).
Trypillia-Cucuteni Dniester-Prut-Bug estimates (3500BC) are derived from
promontory and concentric settlements across the Upper and Middle
Dniester, the Horyn flint-mining zone, and the Southern Bug, including
Stina (3 000), Bilche-Zolote (Verteba Cave, 2 500), Koshylivtsi (2 500),
Zalishchyky (2 200), Nemyriv (2 200), Bodaki (2 000), Klishchiv (2 000),
Horodnytsia (1 800), and Shypyntsi (1 800)
[\[5\]](#ref-videiko2004trypillia). Scythian West Podolian giant
hillfort and Chernyakhov estimates (500BC - 300AD) are derived from the
110 ha earthen rampart enclosure at Nemyriv (4 500), Severynivka (2
500), and Hryhorivka (2 200), and from the Roman-era Chernyakhov,
Lipitsa, and Wielbark craft centres at Lepesivka (1 500), Ripniv II (1
400), Bovshiv (1 200), and Cherneliv-Ruskyi (1 100)
[\[6\]](#ref-smirnova1990nemyriv)[\[7\]](#ref-magomedov2001chernyakhov).

For Galician-Volhynian fortified enclosures (*gorodishcha*) and suburbs
(*posady*, 800AD - 1240AD), following the urban archaeological syntheses
of P. P. Tolochko
[\[8\]](#ref-tolochko1989goroda)[\[9\]](#ref-tolochko2001demography), A.
V. Kuza [\[10\]](#ref-kuza1989malye)[\[11\]](#ref-kuza1996gorodishcha),
V. M. Petehyrych [\[12\]](#ref-petehyrych2006galicia), B. P. Tomenchuk
[\[13\]](#ref-tomenchuk2006halych), and S. V. Tersky
[\[14\]](#ref-tersky2006lutsk), fortified towns are partitioned into the
inner princely citadel (*detinets*), outer rampart enclosure (*okolnyi
grad*), and unfortified artisan quarters (*posady* and *pidhoroddia*).
Compact multi-rampart capitals such as Volodymyr (75 ha, 22 000),
Zvenyhorod (45 ha, 10 000), Lutsk (40 ha, 10 000), Peremyshl (38 ha, 9
000), Kholm (36 ha, 8 000 in 1240AD), Terebovlia (30 ha, 6 500), Belz
(28 ha, 6 000), Bakota (26 ha, 5 500), Peresopnytsia (24 ha, 5 000),
Dorogobuzh-on-Horyn (22 ha, 4 500), Kamianets (22 ha, 4 000), and
Kremenets (20 ha, 4 000) reflect mean urban densities of 200 - 290
persons/ha across combined *detinets* and core *posad* areas.
Conversely, polycentric complexes enclosing meadows, monasteries, and
river ports, notably Halych (*Krylos*, 200 ha, 30 000, 150 persons/ha),
Vasyliv (35 ha, 5 000, 143 persons/ha), and the early tribal enclosures
of Plisnesk (160 ha) and Stilsko (200 ha), reflect lower overall
enclosure densities
[\[13\]](#ref-tomenchuk2006halych)[\[15\]](#ref-fylypchuk2012plisnesk).

For the Mongol conquest destruction horizons and Kingdom of Rus
restructuring (1240AD - 1300AD), estimates are calibrated to the
1240-1241AD archaeological destruction layers excavated at Iziaslavl (2
800 in 1200AD to 600 in 1250AD) [\[16\]](#ref-karger1964iziaslavl),
Kolodyazhyn (2 200 to 450), Zvenyhorod (10 000 to 2 500), Halych (30 000
to 10 000), and Volodymyr (22 000 to 12 000), contrasted against the
unconquered cliff citadels of Kremenets (4 000 in 1200AD to 4 500 in
1250AD and 5 500 in 1300AD) and Danyliv, alongside King Daniel and Lev
Danilovich’s new royal capitals at Kholm (12 000 in 1300AD) and Lviv (12
000 in 1300AD)
[\[17\]](#ref-kotlyar2002chronicle)[\[18\]](#ref-rappoport1967military).
