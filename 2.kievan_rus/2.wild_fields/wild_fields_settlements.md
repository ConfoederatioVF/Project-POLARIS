# Gazetteer Methodology, Schema, and Five-Part Sub-Regional Scope

## Scope and Settlement Data Architecture

This companion settlement gazetteer documents r length(settlements_list)
individual archaeological, proto-urban, fortified, and medieval urban
settlements located across the ~238 000 km² domain of the Wild Fields
(partitioned into five natural sub-regions: I: Budjak
\[Danube-Prut-Dniester Steppe and Liman\], II: NW Pontic Steppe
\[Dniester-Bug Interfluve\], III: Right-Bank Bug-Dnieper and
Ingul-Ingulets, IV: Left-Bank Lower Dnieper, Veliky Lug, and Northern
Taurida, and V: Middle Seversky Donets, Donets Ridge, and Azov Basin)
between 10000BC and 1400AD.

Every population entry records a positive integer (\> 0) anchored to
archaeological site area and dwelling counts, classical *polis* and
*chora* surveys, *Dridu* and *Saltovo-Mayaki* fortification enclosures,
Kievan Rus chronicle and archaeological evidence, and Golden Horde,
Genoese, and Moldavian port records up to 1400AD
[\[1\]](#ref-kryzhitsky1999)[\[2\]](#ref-subbotin2000)[\[3\]](#ref-kravchenko2020)[\[4\]](#ref-dovzhenok1961)[\[5\]](#ref-bocharov2015)[\[6\]](#ref-boguslavskiy2014).
Zero values are omitted; when a settlement is abandoned or destroyed
(for example, Sredny Stog after 3500BC, Olbia after 375AD, Sidorovo
after 965AD, or Kuchugury in 1395AD), its time series terminates cleanly
at its final attested benchmark. All coordinates are verified in decimal
degrees (45,32°N to 50,06°N, 28,29°E to 39,55°E), and all historical and
alternate toponyms are transliterated into Latin script.

# Chronological Settlement Directories (10000BC - 1400AD)

## Prehistoric Forager Camps, Eneolithic Enclosures, and Bronze Age Villages (10000BC - 1000BC)

Table lists all r cnt_prehist prehistoric sites first attested between
10000BC and 1000BC, spanning the Epigravettian bison camps, Budjak
Mesolithic camps (Mirnoe), Dnieper Rapids cemeteries, Bolgrad-Aldeni
(Gumelnita) and Usatovo Eneolithic centres, Yamnaya fortified citadels,
and Sabatinovka-Srubnaya stone-built villages and copper-mining
settlements
[\[2\]](#ref-subbotin2000)[\[7\]](#ref-olenkovskiy2010)[\[8\]](#ref-telegin1986)[\[9\]](#ref-chernienko2014).

## Classical Greek Poleis, Royal Scythia, Late Scythian Fortresses, and Chernyakhov Centres (700BC - 400AD)

Table details the r cnt_classic settlements first attested during the
Classical Greek, Scythian, Sarmatian, Late Scythian, and
Gothic-Chernyakhov horizons (700BC - 400AD) across Budjak, the
Dniester-Bug Liman, and the Dnieper-Donets steppes
[\[10\]](#ref-kryzhitsky1989)[\[11\]](#ref-grakov1954)[\[12\]](#ref-bylkova2010)[\[13\]](#ref-gudkova1999).

## Medieval Steppe Strongholds, Saltovo-Mayaki Towns, Kievan Rus Emporia, and Golden Horde and Genoese Ports (500AD - 1400AD)

Table presents all r cnt_med + cnt_horde settlements first attested
between 500AD and 1400AD, encompassing the Antae/Penkovka rapids
villages, the Voznesenka princely camp, the *Dridu* villages of Budjak,
the *Saltovo-Mayaki* urban network on the Seversky Donets, the Kievan
Rus ports of Oleshye and Protolche, the Polovtsian winter capitals, and
the Golden Horde, Genoese, Moldavian, and Lithuanian cities of Aq Qerman
(Maurocastro), Kiliia (Lycostomo), Smil (Izmail), Kuchugury,
Mamai-Surka, Khadzhibey, and Dashov
[\[3\]](#ref-kravchenko2020)[\[4\]](#ref-dovzhenok1961)[\[5\]](#ref-bocharov2015)[\[6\]](#ref-boguslavskiy2014).
