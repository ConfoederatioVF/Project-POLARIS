# Gazetteer Overview and Summary (172 Sites, 10000BC - 1300AD)

This supplementary settlement gazetteer covers North-Western Rus
(Polotsk, Turov, and Belarusian Rus), strictly book-ended at the Mongol
Conquests (1238-1241AD) and the immediate post-conquest devolved
principalities (1240AD - 1300AD). The gazetteer catalogues 172 distinct
pre-Mongol chronicle towns, fortified *gorodishcha*, and prehistoric and
Iron Age archaeological type-sites across all five sub-regional
clusters.

All population entries in the regional settlement database use strictly
chronological benchmark keys from 10000BC to 1300AD and store strictly
positive integers (\> 0).

# Estimation Methodology by Historical Horizon (10000BC - 1300AD)

## Prehistoric, Neolithic, Bronze Age, and Iron Age Sites (10000BC - 500AD)

For the 24 prehistoric and Iron Age type-sites, site populations are
derived from excavated dwelling counts, cultural layer area (m² or ha),
and palaeodemographic household multipliers (5 - 7 persons per hearth or
pit-dwelling) established by M. M. Charniauski, U. F. Isaenko, A. G.
Mitrofanov, and L. D. Pobol
[\[1\]](#ref-charniauski1997arkhealogiya)[\[2\]](#ref-mitrofanov1978zhelezny)[\[3\]](#ref-pobol1983arkheologicheskie)[\[4\]](#ref-isaenko1999arkhealogiya).
Across Final Palaeolithic and Mesolithic camps (10000BC - 5000BC),
*Yurevichi* (51,9442°N, 29,5314°E) on the Pripyat, *Berdizh* (52,8331°N,
30,9325°E) and *Grensk* (52,9711°N, 30,8922°E) on the Sozh, *Korma*,
*Ankovichi*, and *Borok* represent reindeer- and mammoth-hunter base
camps of 40 - 65 residents at 10000BC, expanding to 70 - 110 inhabitants
in Mesolithic lacustrine settlements at 5000BC. During the Neolithic
pile-dwelling and flint-mining horizon (5000BC - 2500BC), the
waterlogged pile-dwelling cluster of Asavets (*Krivina peat bog*,
54,9614°N, 29,4231°E), Kryvina 1, and the Krasnoselsky flint-mining
complex (53,2642°N, 24,4319°E) supported semi-permanent communities of
160 - 340 inhabitants.

In the Bronze Age and Iron Age hillfort era (1200BC - 500AD), large
lowland enclosures of the Milograd and Zarubintsy cultures in southern
Belarus (*Milograd* 480 at 500BC, *Chaplin* 560 at 200AD, *Goroshkov*
490 at 200AD, *Mokhov* 450 at 500AD) outsized the smaller fortified
promontory hillforts of the Brushed Pottery and Dnieper-Dvina cultures
(*Malyshki* 350 at 200AD, *Zazony* 320 at 200AD, *Bancerovshchina* 490
at 500AD). Located at the confluence of the Menka and Dunai streams 16
km west of modern Minsk, the *Menka* complex (1,5 ha ramparted hillfort
and 40,0 ha open craft settlement excavated by E. M. Zagorulsky) served
as the initial tribal and early Rurikid centre of *Mensk* (850 in 800AD,
1 600 in 900AD, peaking at 2 400 in 1000AD) before the 1067AD Battle on
the Nemiga River and the transfer of the princely seat to the
Svisloch-Nemiga *Zamchishche* at Minsk, declining thereafter to an
unfortified rural *selyshche* (950 in 1100AD; 500 in 1200AD; 420 in
1300AD) [\[5\]](#ref-zagorulsky1982vozniknovenie).

## Pre-Mongol Kievan Rus and Post-1240AD Principality Centres (800AD - 1300AD)

For the 148 fortified medieval towns and *gorodishcha* active between
800AD and 1300AD, urban populations are calibrated from archaeologically
surveyed fortified areas (*Detinets* or *Zamchishche* plus *Okolny
Gorod*) and unfortified craft suburbs (*Posady*), applying the homestead
density models of G. V. Shtykhov (1975, 1992), S. V. Tarasov (1998), P.
F. Lysenko (1974, 1985, 2004), E. M. Zagorulsky (1982, 2001), F. D.
Gurevich (1981), Ya. G. Zverugo (1989), and A. V. Kuza (1989, 1996),
which estimate 110 - 150 persons/ha inside densely built timber-street
*detinets* and *okolny gorod* quarters and 60 - 90 persons/ha across
outer *posady*
[\[6\]](#ref-shtykhov1975drevniy)[\[7\]](#ref-shtykhov1992goroda)[\[8\]](#ref-tarasov1998polotsk)[\[9\]](#ref-lysenko1974goroda)[\[10\]](#ref-lysenko1985berestye)[\[11\]](#ref-kuza1989malye)[\[12\]](#ref-kuza1996drevnerusskie).
