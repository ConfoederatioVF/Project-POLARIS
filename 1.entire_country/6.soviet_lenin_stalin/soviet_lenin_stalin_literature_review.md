# Population Estimates, Censuses, and the 45 Stalin-Era Regions (1926AD - 1953AD)

Between the First All-Union Census of 17 December 1926AD and the death
of Joseph Stalin on 5 March 1953AD, the territories of the Russian SFSR
(including Kaliningrad Oblast from 1945AD and the Karelo-Finnish SSR
from 1940AD to 1956AD), the Ukrainian SSR, the Byelorussian SSR, the
Estonian, Latvian, and Lithuanian SSRs, and the Moldavian SSR
experienced the most violent demographic convulsions, forced population
movements, and ethno-territorial restructurings in modern Eurasian
history
[\[1\]](#ref-andreev1993naselenie)[\[2\]](#ref-andreev1998demograficheskaya)[\[3\]](#ref-davies2004years)[\[4\]](#ref-polian2001against).
Over twenty-seven years, the population of these forty-five regions was
reshaped by seven interlocking shocks.

Forced Collectivisation and Dekulakisation (1929-1933AD): The
expropriation and freight-train exile of 1 803 392 peasants (381 026
families in 1930-1931AD, rising to ~2,1M by 1933AD) as special settlers
(spetspereselentsy) into northern, Ural, and Siberian forestry, mining,
and taiga clearings ([archived
PDF](papers/soviet_kulak_spetspereselentsy_2023_1.pdf); [archived
PDF](papers/soviet_kulak_spetspereselentsy_2014_2.pdf))
[\[5\]](#ref-zemskov1991spetsposelentsy)[\[6\]](#ref-zemskov2003spetsposelentsy)[\[7\]](#ref-werth2007cannibal).

The 1932-1933AD Man-Made Famines (Holodomor in Ukraine, MASSR, Kuban,
and Volga): Direct famine mortality of 3,5M to 3,94M in the Ukrainian
SSR and Moldavian ASSR (4,53M including lost births) and ~2,63M across
the Russian SFSR (Kuban, Don, Lower and Middle Volga, Volga German ASSR,
and Central Black-Earth Belt) ([archived
PDF](papers/soviet_kuban_volga_famine_1933_2009_1.pdf); [archived
PDF](papers/soviet_kuban_volga_famine_1933_2026_2.pdf))
[\[8\]](#ref-rudnytskyi2015holodomor)[\[9\]](#ref-wolowyna2020holodomor)[\[10\]](#ref-kondrashin2008golod)[\[11\]](#ref-nazarenko2026northcaucasus1933).

Pre-War Border Cleansings (1935-1938AD), the 1937AD Korean Deportation,
and the 1937-1938AD Great Terror: NKVD expulsions of Poles, Germans, and
Ingrian Finns from western border zones, the total deportation of 171
781 Soviet Koreans from Primorye (Region 45) in autumn 1937AD, and the
execution of ~681 692 persons during the 1937-1938AD Yezhovshchina and
national operations
[\[4\]](#ref-polian2001against)[\[12\]](#ref-martin2001affirmative)[\[13\]](#ref-khlevniuk2004history).

Stalinist Five-Year-Plan Industrialisation and the Gulag Archipelago
(1928-1953AD): The rural-to-urban transfer of \>25M peasants into
heavy-industrial metropolises (Regions 12, 13, 17, 20, 23-25, 34, 35,
37) alongside forced-labour colonisation of the Arctic, sub-Arctic, and
Far East (Belbaltlag, Vorkutlag, Ukhtpechlag, Dmitlag, Siblag, Norillag,
Ozerlag, Bamlag, and Dalstroy/Sevvostlag across Regions 16, 18, 20, 36,
37, 40-45) ([archived PDF](papers/soviet_gulag_demography_2022_1.pdf);
[archived PDF](papers/soviet_urbanisation_five_year_plans_2019_1.pdf))
[\[14\]](#ref-kustyshev2022kolagulag)[\[15\]](#ref-senyavsky2019urbanisation)[\[16\]](#ref-applebaum2003gulag).

The 1939-1940AD Western Annexations (Baltic SSRs, Bessarabia, Western
Belarus, Western Ukraine, and Finnish Karelia): Molotov-Ribbentrop Pact
occupation of Western Belarus (Region 6), Galicia-Volhynia (Region 8),
Finnish Karelia (Region 16), Bessarabia/Northern Bukovina (Regions 4 and
9), and the Estonian, Latvian, and Lithuanian SSRs (Regions 1-3),
accompanied by the 1939-1941AD Umsiedlung of ~215k Baltic and
Bessarabian Germans and the June 1941AD NKVD deportations ([archived
PDF](papers/soviet_lenin_stalin_baltic_priboi_rahi_tamm_2008.pdf);
[archived
PDF](papers/soviet_lenin_stalin_moldavia_massr_bancila_2023.pdf))
[\[17\]](#ref-mertelsmann2009estonia)[\[18\]](#ref-strods2002priboi)[\[19\]](#ref-anusauskas1996teroras)[\[20\]](#ref-casu2014moldova).

The Great Patriotic War (1941-1945AD), the Holocaust, and Total Ethnic
Deportations (1941-1944AD): Wartime losses of 26,6M to 27,0M across the
USSR, the Holocaust of ~2,5M to 2,7M Jews across the occupied Soviet
republics (including Ponary, Ninth Fort, Rumbula, Minsk, Babyn Yar, and
Transnistria), the 872-day Siege of Leningrad (Region 17), eastward
factory/civilian evacuation, and the liquidation of the Volga German
ASSR (Region 26), Karachay AO (Region 30), Kalmyk ASSR (Region 28),
Chechen-Ingush ASSR (Region 32), Balkar districts (Region 31), and
Crimean ASSR (Region 15) ([archived
PDF](papers/soviet_ethnic_deportations_1941_1944_2005_1.pdf); [archived
PDF](papers/soviet_lenin_stalin_moldavia_holocaust_massr_shapiro_2021.pdf))
[\[21\]](#ref-bugai1995deportatsiya)[\[22\]](#ref-pohl1999ethnic)[\[23\]](#ref-arad2009holocaust)[\[24\]](#ref-shapiro2021transnistria).

Post-War Expulsions, the 1946-1947AD Famine, and 1948-1951AD Borderland
Deportations (Vesna, Priboi, Yug, Osen): The total population
replacement of Kaliningrad Oblast (Region 5) and South Sakhalin (Region
45), Polish-Ukrainian-Lithuanian population transfers, fierce
anti-Soviet partisan wars in Lithuania, Latvia, Estonia, and Western
Ukraine, the 1946-1947AD Soviet famine (peaking per capita in the
Moldavian SSR, Region 4), and mass collectivisation deportations across
the Baltic and Moldavian SSRs ([archived
PDF](papers/soviet_kaliningrad_repopulation_1945_2022_1.pdf); [archived
PDF](papers/soviet_lenin_stalin_moldavia_famine_casu_2020.pdf))
[\[25\]](#ref-kostyashov2022kaliningrad)[\[26\]](#ref-ellman2000famine1947)[\[27\]](#ref-casu2020famine1947)[\[28\]](#ref-statiev2010borderlands).

## Archival Census Benchmarks, Epoch Boundary Splicing, and Reconstructed 45-Region Totals (1926AD - 1953AD)

To eliminate anachronistic post-1991AD administrative boundaries and
capture the exact territorial footprint of Stalinist demographic
engineering, this synthesis partitions the European and Asian USSR
(excluding Transcaucasia and Central Asia, which are modelled in their
respective regional series) into 45 regions spanning 18 177 917 km2
across eleven benchmark years (1926, 1930, 1933, 1937, 1939, 1941, 1943,
1945, 1947, 1950, 1953)
[\[1\]](#ref-andreev1993naselenie)[\[2\]](#ref-andreev1998demograficheskaya)[\[29\]](#ref-polyakov1991perepis1937)[\[30\]](#ref-polyakov1992perepis1939):

17 December 1926AD First All-Union Census and Epoch Splicing (145 070k
across the 45 regions): Published in 56 volumes under Vasiliy
Mikhaylovsky (TsSU SSSR), the 1926AD census enumerated 147,03M across
the interwar USSR (92,74M in the RSFSR, 29,02M in the UkSSR including
572k in the Moldavian ASSR, and 4,98M in the BSSR)
[\[31\]](#ref-tsunkhu1929perepis1926). Within our constant 1953AD outer
envelope of 45 regions (which excludes interwar Congress Poland and
mainland Finland covered in the preceding 1917-1926AD epoch at 167 780k,
while incorporating the 1926AD baselines of Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania,
Bessarabia, Northern East Prussia/Kaliningrad, Galicia-Volhynia,
Northern Bukovina, Transcarpathia, Finnish Viipuri/Karelia, Tannu-Tuva,
and Southern Sakhalin), the 1926AD baseline stood at 145 070k: 5 620k
across the three Baltic republics (1 115k in Estonia Region 1, 1 860k in
Latvia Region 2, and 2 645k in Lithuania Region 3), 2 040k in Moldavia
Region 4 (Bessarabia plus left-bank MASSR), 7 180k in Belarus Regions
6-7, 36 700k in Ukraine/Crimea Regions 8-15, and 93 530k in
Russia/Karelia/Kaliningrad (1 120k in Northern East Prussia Region 5 and
92 410k across Regions 16-45). High late-NEP natural increase raised the
45-region total to a pre-famine peak of 153 848k in 1930AD.

The 1933AD Famine Trough (149 934k) and the Suppressed 6 January 1937AD
Census (157 315k): Collectivisation and the 1932-1933AD famines reduced
the 45-region total by -3 914k between 1930AD and 1933AD (149 934k: 5
874k in the Baltic states, 2 190k in Moldavia where MASSR famine losses
were offset by interwar Romanian Bessarabian growth, 7 700k in Belarus,
35 350k in Ukraine/Crimea - a net collapse of -3 650k driven by -3 900k
across Soviet Ukrainian Regions 10-14 - and 98 820k in
Russia/Karelia/Kaliningrad)
[\[8\]](#ref-rudnytskyi2015holodomor)[\[10\]](#ref-kondrashin2008golod).
When the one-day de facto All-Union Census of 6 January 1937AD recorded
only 162,04M across the USSR (~8M below Stalin’s proclaimed target), the
Sovnarkom declared the census “wrecked”, executed or imprisoned its
statistical leadership (I. A. Kraval, O. A. Kvitkin, M. V. Kurman, L. S.
Brandgendler), and locked the returns in RGAE (Fond 1562, Opis 329)
until 1989-1991AD ([archived
PDF](papers/soviet_ukraine_demography_1926_1939_2013_2.pdf))
[\[29\]](#ref-polyakov1991perepis1937)[\[32\]](#ref-zhiromskaya1996polveka)[\[33\]](#ref-krasnobaeva2013statistics1930s).
Across the 45 regions, the 1937AD population stood at 157 315k.

The 17 January 1939AD Census (166 199k) and June 1941AD Pre-Invasion
Peak (172 579k): Declassified audits of the 1939AD census (170,56M
official USSR total) by Andreev, Darsky, and Kharkova (1993, 1998)
demonstrated a central pripiska (statistical inflation) of 1,7M to 2,9M
and a secret redistribution of ~760k to 850k NKVD camp inmates from
northern and Siberian Gulag regions (Regions 16, 18, 36, 37, 40-45) to
rural districts of Ukraine and Kazakhstan to conceal both Gulag
concentrations and 1933AD famine deficits ([archived
PDF](papers/soviet_1937_1939_census_2023_1.pdf); [archived
PDF](papers/soviet_1937_1939_census_2013_2.pdf))
[\[1\]](#ref-andreev1993naselenie)[\[30\]](#ref-polyakov1992perepis1939)[\[34\]](#ref-amirkhanova2023dagestan)[\[35\]](#ref-dmitrienko2013tomsk1939).
Harmonised across all 45 regions, total population reached 166 199k in
1939AD and peaked in June 1941AD at 172 579k (5 994k in the Baltic SSRs
after the 1939-1941AD Baltic German Umsiedlung and 14 June 1941AD
deportations, 2 470k in the Moldavian SSR, 8 800k in the Byelorussian
SSR, 41 560k in the Ukrainian SSR including Crimea, and 113 755k in
Russia/Karelia/East Prussia).

The 1943AD Wartime Midpoint (146 480k), 1945AD Post-War Trough (142
619k), 1947AD Famine (146 240k), and 1953AD Late Stalinist Splice (165
350k): Nazi/Romanian occupation, combat mortality, the Holocaust, urban
sieges, wartime flight, and 1941-1944AD ethnic deportations depressed
the 45-region total to 146 480k in 1943AD and 142 619k in 1945AD (-29
960k, or -17,4%, below June 1941AD: 4 894k in the Baltic SSRs, 2 080k in
the Moldavian SSR, 180k in devastated Kaliningrad Oblast, 6 300k in
Belarus, 30 150k in Ukraine/Crimea, and 99 015k across Regions 16-45)
([archived PDF](papers/soviet_wwii_losses_demography_2007_1.pdf);
[archived PDF](papers/soviet_belarus_wwii_demography_2004_1.pdf))
[\[36\]](#ref-golotik2007wwiilosses)[\[37\]](#ref-bylov2004belarus).
Checked by the 1946-1947AD famine (146 240k in 1947AD, when the
Moldavian SSR plunged to its 1 940k famine trough) and 1947-1951AD
western deportations, post-war demobilisation and industrial
reconstruction carried the 45 regions to 156 532k in 1950AD and 165 350k
in March 1953AD (5 780k in the Baltic SSRs, 2 520k in the Moldavian SSR,
7 200k in the Byelorussian SSR, 35 480k in the Ukrainian SSR including
Crimea, and 114 370k in the RSFSR including 540k in Kaliningrad and 1
120k in the Karelo-Finnish SSR), splicing identically at 165 350k with
the subsequent post-Stalin (1953AD - 1991AD) epoch.

# Collectivisation, Dekulakisation (1930-1933AD), and the 1932-1933AD Famines (Holodomor, Kuban, and Volga)

## Origin-to-Destination Accounting of Kulak Special Settlers (Spetspereselentsy) and the May 1933AD Nazino Tragedy

The demographic rupture of the 1930s commenced in November 1929AD with
comprehensive forced collectivisation (sploshnaya kollektivizatsiya) and
the Politburo resolution of 30 January 1930AD (“On Measures for the
Elimination of Kulak Households in Districts of Comprehensive
Collectivisation”)
[\[3\]](#ref-davies2004years)[\[6\]](#ref-zemskov2003spetsposelentsy).
Peasant households were divided into three categories: first-category
“counter-revolutionaries” (~63k household heads arrested by the OGPU in
1930AD, many executed or sent to concentration camps), second-category
households slated for freight-train deportation to remote northern,
Ural, and Siberian special settlements (spetsposelki), and
third-category households dispossessed and resettled on marginal plots
within their home districts ([archived
PDF](papers/soviet_kulak_spetspereselentsy_2023_1.pdf); [archived
PDF](papers/soviet_kulak_spetspereselentsy_2014_2.pdf))
[\[5\]](#ref-zemskov1991spetsposelentsy)[\[38\]](#ref-isyangulov2023kulak)[\[39\]](#ref-evseev2014southurals).

Declassified OGPU-NKVD ledgers compiled by Viktor Zemskov (1990, 2003)
establish that during the two main waves of 1930-1931AD, 381 026 peasant
families comprising 1 803 392 persons were exiled into “kulak special
settlements” (kulatskaya ssylka); including 1932-1933AD supplemental
deportations (such as blacklisted Kuban Cossack stanitsas and Western
Siberian/Ukrainian arrears sweeps), total dekulakised exiles reached
~2,1M
[\[4\]](#ref-polian2001against)[\[5\]](#ref-zemskov1991spetsposelentsy)[\[6\]](#ref-zemskov2003spetsposelentsy).
By region of origin, the 1 803 392 spetspereselentsy of 1930-1931AD were
uprooted primarily from the Ukrainian SSR (Regions 10-14, 63 720
families, ~301 500 persons), the North Caucasus and Kuban-Don (Regions
29-32, 38 404 families, ~191 900 persons), the Lower and Middle Volga
(Regions 24-27, 47 264 families, ~230 500 persons, including ~26k Volga
Germans from Region 26), the Central Black-Earth Belt (Region 22, 26 006
families, ~130 000 persons), the Urals and Bashkiria (Region 34, 35 450
families, ~172 000 persons), Western and Eastern Siberia (Regions 35-40,
68 500 families, ~335 000 persons), and Eastern Belarus (Region 7, 15
724 families, ~76 800 persons)
[\[6\]](#ref-zemskov2003spetsposelentsy)[\[38\]](#ref-isyangulov2023kulak).

Across our 45-region partition, these exiles were consigned to seven
primary reception zones (Table ). Region 34 (Great Ural Arsenal &
Bashkir ASSR): Received the largest single contingent (117 195 families,
561 429 persons in 1930-1931AD), deployed to timber camps
(lespromkhozy), bauxite and potash mines (Nadezhdinsk/Serov, Solikamsk,
Berezniki, Kizel), and the blast-furnace foundations of Magnitogorsk
(40k+ special settlers) and Nizhny Tagil
[\[39\]](#ref-evseev2014southurals)[\[40\]](#ref-kotkin1995magnetic).

Region 19 (Northern Forestry Belt: Arkhangelsk & Vologda) and Region 18
(Komi ASSR: Ukhtpechlag): Received 57 716 families (290 025 persons) in
1930-1931AD, distributed across logging clearings along the Northern
Dvina, Vychegda, and Pechora rivers (Kotlas, Pinega, Syktyvkar,
Ust-Kulom, and Ukhta), where winter exposure and scurvy killed over 45k
deportees in 1930-1932AD [\[6\]](#ref-zemskov2003spetsposelentsy).

Region 36 (Ob-Irtysh Special-Settlement Taiga & Tundra: Narym Okrug,
Ostyak-Vogul/Yugra & Yamal-Nenets Okrugs): Received ~265 000 kulak
exiles and “declassed elements” shipped by barge down the Ob, Irtysh,
Tom, Vasyugan, and Parabel rivers into the swamps of Narym Okrug (~196
000 settlers across Parabel, Kargasok, Kolpashevo, and Vasyugan) and the
newly created (December 1930AD) Ostyak-Vogul (Khanty-Mansi) and
Yamal-Nenets National Okrugs (Samarovo/Khanty-Mansiysk, Surgut,
Berezovo, Salekhard)
[\[6\]](#ref-zemskov2003spetsposelentsy)[\[7\]](#ref-werth2007cannibal).
This forced influx drove Region 36 up by +33,3% from 360k in 1926AD to
480k in 1930AD and +29,2% to 620k in 1933AD (Figure ). In May 1933AD,
the Nazino Island Tragedy unfolded at the confluence of the Ob and
Nazina rivers in Alexandrovsky Raion (Region 36): some 6 700 “socially
harmful elements” and famine refugees rounded up in Moscow and Leningrad
under passport-cleansing quotas were marooned without food, tools, or
shelter on swampy Nazino Island; within thirteen weeks, more than 4 000
perished from starvation, exposure, typhus, and cannibalism
[\[7\]](#ref-werth2007cannibal).

Region 37 (Kuzbass Coal-Steel Combine & Siblag) and Region 39 (Khakass
AO & Minusinsk): Received ~145 000 special settlers assigned to coal
mines at Prokopyevsk, Anzhero-Sudzhensk, Leninsk-Kuznetsky, and
Kemerovo, the Kuznetsk Metallurgical Combine at Stalinsk, and gold/coal
mines in Khakassia (Chernogorsk, Balyksinsk, Sarala)
[\[6\]](#ref-zemskov2003spetsposelentsy).

Regions 40, 41, 43, and 45 (East Siberia, Yenisei-Taymyr Igarka, Aldan
Gold & Far East): Received ~185 000 special settlers, notably at the
Arctic timber-export port of Igarka (Region 41, where \>20k kulak exiles
built sawmills on permafrost at 67,5°N), the Aldan goldfields (Region
43), and Far Eastern mining and timber districts (Region 45). Between
1930AD and 1934AD, recorded deaths in the kulak special settlements
exceeded 500 000 (151 601 deaths, or 13,7% of the entire contingent, in
the famine year 1933AD alone) [\[6\]](#ref-zemskov2003spetsposelentsy).

## The 1932-1933AD Famines across Ukrainian Regions 10-14, MASSR, and Russian Regions 22, 24-30

In the Ukrainian SSR, the combination of collectivisation livestock
losses, extortionate 1932AD state grain-procurement quotas
(khlebozagotovki), “natural meat and potato fines” (naturalnye shtrafy),
the “blackboard” blockade regime (chernye doski), and Stalin and
Molotov’s secret directive of 22 January 1933AD forbidding peasant exit
from Ukraine and the Kuban produced the Holodomor
[\[8\]](#ref-rudnytskyi2015holodomor)[\[9\]](#ref-wolowyna2020holodomor)[\[41\]](#ref-mesle2012mortalite).
Reconstructions by the Ptoukha Institute and the Harvard Ukrainian
Research Institute (HURI) MAPA project establish 3,94M direct excess
deaths across the interwar Ukrainian SSR (including the Moldavian ASSR)
during 1932-1934AD (3,53M in 1933AD alone) plus 0,59M lost births (4,53M
total demographic loss)
[\[8\]](#ref-rudnytskyi2015holodomor)[\[9\]](#ref-wolowyna2020holodomor).

Our 45-region partition cleanly separates the interwar Polish, Romanian,
Baltic, and Czechoslovak borderlands (Regions 1-3, the Bessarabian
majority of Region 4, and Regions 6, 8, and 9, which were outside Soviet
borders in 1932-1933AD) from the five Soviet Ukrainian regions (Regions
10 through 14) and the Russian famine regions (Regions 22, 24-30)
(Figure , and Table ). Region 10 (Right-Bank Ukrainian SSR: Kyiv,
Vinnytsia, Zhytomyr, Cherkasy): Refined by assigning the Moldavian SSR
to dedicated Region 4, Right-Bank Ukraine suffered rural excess
mortality rates of 165 to 250+ per 1 000 in Tetiev, Bila Tserkva, Uman,
and Vinnytsia raions, plunging by -18,1% from 8 560k (1930AD) to 7 010k
(1933AD, -1 550k net loss; ~1 650k direct famine deaths)
[\[9\]](#ref-wolowyna2020holodomor). Meanwhile, in the left-bank
Moldavian ASSR (Balta and Tiraspol districts, split in 1940AD between
Region 4 and Region 14), ~50k to 67k inhabitants died during the
1932-1933AD famine ([archived
PDF](papers/soviet_lenin_stalin_moldavia_massr_bancila_2023.pdf))
[\[20\]](#ref-casu2014moldova)[\[42\]](#ref-bancila2023massr).

Region 11 (Left-Bank Ukrainian SSR: Kharkiv, Poltava, Sumy, Chernihiv):
Subjected to sweeping household food confiscations and chernye doski
blockades (such as Liutenka and Kamiani Potoky), plunging by -18,1% from
9 950k (1930AD) to 8 150k (1933AD, -1 800k net loss; ~1 295k direct
famine deaths) [\[8\]](#ref-rudnytskyi2015holodomor).

Region 14 (Ukrainian Black Sea Steppe: Odesa, Mykolaiv, Kherson,
including Balta) and Region 12 (Dnieper Industrial Bend): Region 14 fell
by -12,5% (2 790k to 2 440k; ~395k direct deaths including northern
MASSR raions retained in Odesa Oblast), and Region 12 fell by -10,1% (3
950k to 3 550k; ~370k rural famine deaths partially offset by industrial
construction at Zaporizhzhia’s DniproHES and Kryvyi Rih), whereas Region
13 (Donbas) rose by +5,5% (3 650k to 3 850k) as priority coal/steel
rations drew starving peasants into Stalino and Voroshilovgrad mines
despite ~230k rural Donetsk famine deaths.

Region 26 (Volga German ASSR) and Region 27 (Saratov, Stalingrad &
Astrakhan Lower Volga): Isolated as its own republic in our 45-region
partition, the Volga German ASSR (Region 26) suffered the steepest
percentage drop of any Russian unit in 1930-1933AD, plunging by -20,0%
from 600k (1930AD) to 480k (1933AD) as relentless grain requisitions
across Marxstadt, Balzer, Seelmann, and Frank cantons killed ~65k to 80k
Volga Germans and triggered flight across the Volga
[\[10\]](#ref-kondrashin2008golod)[\[43\]](#ref-german2000nemtsy).
Adjacent Region 27 (Saratov & Stalingrad) fell by -12,7% (4 400k to 3
840k; ~405k direct famine deaths).

Region 29 (Rostov, Krasnodar/Kuban & Adygei AO) and Region 30 (Stavropol
& Karachay-Cherkess): Directed by Lazar Kaganovich’s November 1932AD
North Caucasus commission, the OGPU placed fifteen major Kuban Cossack
stanitsas on “blackboards” (chernye doski), cordoned them off from trade
and food, and deported the entire surviving population of
Ukrainian-speaking Poltavskaya (~27k residents, renamed
Krasnoarmeyskaya), Medvedovskaya, and Urupskaya to the northern Gulag
while resettling demobilised Red Army soldiers into their homes
([archived PDF](papers/soviet_kuban_volga_famine_1933_2026_2.pdf))
[\[10\]](#ref-kondrashin2008golod)[\[11\]](#ref-nazarenko2026northcaucasus1933).
Region 29 plunged by -15,4% from 7 420k (1930AD) to 6 280k (1933AD),
while Region 30 (Stavropol) fell by -10,6% (1 700k to 1 520k; ~620k
combined North Caucasus famine deaths).

Region 22 (Central Black-Earth Belt), Region 24 (Tatar ASSR &
Ulyanovsk-Penza), and Region 25 (Kuibyshev & Chkalov): Region 22 dropped
by -6,3% (14 350k to 13 450k; ~520k direct famine deaths in Voronezh,
Kursk, Belgorod, and Tambov), Region 25 fell by -5,2% (3 850k to 3
650k), and Region 24 fell by -3,7% (5 350k to 5 150k) ([archived
PDF](papers/soviet_famine_1932_1933_2023_2.pdf); [archived
PDF](papers/soviet_famine_1932_1933_2023_1.pdf))
[\[44\]](#ref-nazarenko2023chernozem1933)[\[45\]](#ref-nazarenko2023bashkiria1933)[\[46\]](#ref-bublikov2017belgorod).

across Ukrainian and Russian famine regions from 1930AD to 1933AD.

# Western Soviet Annexations (Regions 1 - 5): The Baltic SSRs, Moldavian SSR, and Kaliningrad Oblast (1926AD - 1953AD)

The forty-five-region partition incorporates five western borderland
territories whose demographic trajectories between 1926AD and 1953AD
were transformed by the August 1939AD Molotov-Ribbentrop secret
protocols, the 1939-1941AD German *Umsiedlung*, the 1940AD Soviet
annexations and June 1941AD NKVD deportations, the 1941-1944AD Nazi and
Romanian occupations and the Holocaust, wartime sea flight and post-war
population expulsions, prolonged anti-Soviet partisan insurgencies, the
catastrophic 1946-1947AD Moldavian famine, and the 1948-1951AD mass
collectivisation deportations (Operations *Vesna*, *Priboi*, *Yug* and
*Osen*) (Table )
[\[4\]](#ref-polian2001against)[\[18\]](#ref-strods2002priboi)[\[20\]](#ref-casu2014moldova)[\[28\]](#ref-statiev2010borderlands)[\[47\]](#ref-kostyashov2009sekretnaya).

## Region 1: Estonian SSR (1 115k in 1926AD -\> 1 134k in 1939AD -\> 854k in 1945AD -\> 1 150k in 1953AD)

In interwar independent Estonia (49 227 km2), low Western European-style
fertility and modest urban growth in Tallinn (127k in 1926AD -\> 145k in
1939AD), Tartu (59k -\> 60,1k) and Narva carried the population slowly
from 1 115k in 1926AD to 1 134k in September 1939AD
[\[48\]](#ref-katus1990estonia)[\[49\]](#ref-parming1972estonia).
Between October 1939AD and June 1941AD, Estonia lost ~80k inhabitants (1
134k -\> 1 054k): following Hitler’s 6 October 1939AD Reichstag call,
~14 000 Baltic Germans departed during the 1939-1940AD *Umsiedlung*
(followed by ~7 000 *Nachumsiedler* in early 1941AD); after the
June-August 1940AD Soviet occupation and annexation, NKVD troops
arrested ~8 000 political and military figures, deported 10 157 men,
women and children on 14 June 1941AD to Kirov Oblast and
Novosibirsk/Tomsk (Region 35-36), and conscripted ~33 000 Estonian men
into Red Army labour battalions in the summer of 1941AD ([archived
PDF](papers/soviet_lenin_stalin_baltic_priboi_rahi_tamm_2008.pdf);
[archived PDF](papers/soviet_lenin_stalin_baltic_priboi_annus_2017.pdf))
[\[17\]](#ref-mertelsmann2009estonia)[\[50\]](#ref-rahitamm2008estonia)[\[51\]](#ref-annus2017estonia).

Under Nazi German occupation (July 1941AD - September 1944AD),
*Einsatzgruppe A* and Estonian collaborationist units murdered virtually
all ~1 000 Estonian Jews remaining in the country (such that Nazi
authorities declared Estonia *judenfrei* at the January 1942AD Wannsee
Conference) alongside ~7 000 to 8 000 foreign Jews transported to the
Vaivara, Klooga and Kalevi-Liiva camps and ~7 800 local civilians and
Roma ([archived
PDF](papers/soviet_lenin_stalin_estonia_latvia_demog_kaasik_2023.pdf))
[\[17\]](#ref-mertelsmann2009estonia)[\[52\]](#ref-kaasik2023estonia).
As the Red Army breached the Narva front (destroying \>95% of baroque
Narva, whose population fell from 23,8k in 1939AD to 6,6k in 1945AD) and
reoccupied Tallinn in autumn 1944AD, 75 000 to 80 000 Estonians
(together with ~7 000 Estonian Swedes from Vormsi, Noarootsi and Ruhnu)
fled across the Baltic Sea to Sweden and Germany
[\[48\]](#ref-katus1990estonia)[\[49\]](#ref-parming1972estonia). In
January 1945AD, Moscow detached eastern Ivangorod and the bulk of
Petseri (*Pechory*) County (~2 300 km2, ~55k-65k inhabitants) from the
Estonian SSR into Leningrad and Pskov Oblasts (Region 17), depressing
Estonia’s post-war trough to 854k in late 1945AD (-24,7% below 1939AD).

Post-war Sovietisation provoked armed resistance by ~14 000 to 15 000
Estonian *Metsavennad* (Forest Brothers) across Virumaa, Vorumaa and
Parnumaa
[\[17\]](#ref-mertelsmann2009estonia)[\[28\]](#ref-statiev2010borderlands).
To crush rural support for the insurgents and force peasant entry into
kolkhozes, MGB and MVD troops executed Operation *Priboi* (“Coastal
Surf”) on 25-28 March 1949AD, deporting 20 702 Estonians (7 552
families, \>70% women and children under sixteen) in 19 freight echelons
to Novosibirsk, Omsk, Krasnoyarsk and Irkutsk Oblasts (Regions 35 and
40) ([archived
PDF](papers/soviet_lenin_stalin_baltic_priboi_rahi_tamm_2008.pdf))
[\[18\]](#ref-strods2002priboi)[\[50\]](#ref-rahitamm2008estonia).
Simultaneously, rapid all-Union investment in the northeastern Virumaa
oil-shale gas basin (Kohtla-Jarve, founded as a city in 1946AD to supply
piped shale gas to Leningrad, growing to 29k by 1953AD), Kreenholm
textiles in Narva (21k in 1953AD, after indigenous pre-war residents
were barred from returning) and the secret Combine No. 7
uranium-enrichment facility at Sillamae (10k in 1953AD) brought \>180
000 Russian, Belarusian and Ingrian industrial migrants into Estonia,
lifting Region 1 to 1 150k by 1953AD while reducing the ethnic Estonian
share from 88,2% (1934AD) to ~75% ([archived
PDF](papers/soviet_lenin_stalin_estonia_latvia_demog_martinson_2002.pdf))
[\[48\]](#ref-katus1990estonia)[\[49\]](#ref-parming1972estonia)[\[53\]](#ref-martinson2002oilshale).

## Region 2: Latvian SSR (1 860k in 1926AD -\> 1 995k in 1939AD -\> 1 580k in 1945AD -\> 2 010k in 1953AD)

Interwar Latvia (64 240 km2) expanded from 1 860k in 1926AD to 1 995k in
1939AD, anchored by Riga (339k -\> 385k), Liepaja (57k), Daugavpils (43k
-\> 51,7k) and Jelgava (28,3k -\> 34,1k)
[\[54\]](#ref-zvidrins1992latvia)[\[55\]](#ref-plakans1995latvians).
Between October 1939AD and June 1941AD, the *Umsiedlung* removed ~51 000
Baltic Germans (plus ~10 500 in early 1941AD), virtually ending seven
centuries of German urban presence in Riga and Courland; following the
June-August 1940AD Soviet annexation, the NKVD deported 15 424 Latvian
citizens (disproportionately targeting political, military and Jewish
business elites) on 14 June 1941AD to Krasnoyarsk Krai (Region 40),
Tomsk/Novosibirsk (Region 35-36) and the Vyatlag/Usollag camps (Regions
23 and 34), where ~34% perished ([archived
PDF](papers/soviet_lenin_stalin_latvia_soviet_2023.pdf))
[\[56\]](#ref-bleiere2006latvia)[\[57\]](#ref-feldentale2023latvia).

During the 1941-1945AD Nazi occupation (*Reichskommissariat Ostland*),
the Latvian Holocaust annihilated ~70 000 Latvian Jews (~90% of the
pre-war Jewish community not evacuated to the Soviet interior) alongside
~20 000 Reich Jews deported from Germany, Austria and Czechoslovakia and
~2 000 Latvian Roma
[\[23\]](#ref-arad2009holocaust)[\[56\]](#ref-bleiere2006latvia). On 30
November and 8 December 1941AD, SS Gen. Friedrich Jeckeln’s forces and
the Arajs Kommando shot 25 000 Jews from the Riga Ghetto in the Rumbula
forest, alongside mass shootings in Bikernieki forest (~35k-46k total
victims), the Skede dunes outside Liepaja, Daugavpils Citadel and
Rezekne [\[23\]](#ref-arad2009holocaust). Heavy conscription into the
Latvian Waffen-SS Legion (~110k-115k) and the Red Army, fierce
1944-1945AD fighting around Jelgava and the Courland Pocket (*Kurzemes
cietoksnis*, which held out until 8 May 1945AD), the flight of 120 000
to 150 000 Latvian refugees by sea to Germany and Sweden, and the August
1944AD transfer of eastern Abrene (*Pytalovo*) district (1 293 km2, ~45k
inhabitants) to RSFSR Pskov Oblast (Region 17) reduced Latvia’s
population by -20,8% from 1 995k (1939AD) to 1 580k in 1945AD
[\[55\]](#ref-plakans1995latvians)[\[56\]](#ref-bleiere2006latvia).

Between 1944AD and 1953AD, ~20 000 Latvian national partisans
(*Nacionalie partizani*) waged guerrilla warfare in the forests of
Kurzeme, Vidzeme and Latgale [\[28\]](#ref-statiev2010borderlands).
During Operation *Priboi* (25-29 March 1949AD), 33 echelons deported 42
149 Latvians (13 624 families - 2,3% of Latvia’s population in four
days) to Amur Oblast (Region 45), Omsk and Tomsk Oblasts (Region 35-36),
triggering instant mass collectivisation (11% to \>90% of farms by late
1949AD)
[\[18\]](#ref-strods2002priboi)[\[57\]](#ref-feldentale2023latvia).
Simultaneously, heavy union-ministry industrialisation in Riga (*VEF*
electronics, *RVR* railcars, shipbuilding; growing from 228k in late
1944/1945AD to 530k in 1953AD), Daugavpils (56k) and Liepaja (64k) drew
\>350 000 Russian, Belarusian and Ukrainian workers and military
personnel, carrying Region 2 to 2 010k by 1953AD while lowering the
ethnic Latvian share from 77% (1935AD) to ~62%
[\[54\]](#ref-zvidrins1992latvia)[\[56\]](#ref-bleiere2006latvia).

## Region 3: Lithuanian SSR (2 645k in 1926AD -\> 3 030k in 1941AD -\> 2 460k in 1945AD -\> 2 620k in 1953AD)

Lithuania (64 449 km2, evaluated across its post-1945AD borders
including both the Vilnius region and Klaipeda/Memel) grew from 2 645k
in 1926AD to 2 980k in 1939AD and 3 030k in early 1941AD
[\[58\]](#ref-balkelis2005vilnius)[\[59\]](#ref-damusis1998lithuania).
Its borders shifted twice before Barbarossa: in March 1939AD Nazi
Germany annexed the Klaipeda (*Memel*) Territory (~154k inhabitants),
whereas in October 1939AD - after invading eastern Poland - the USSR
transferred the city of Vilnius (*Wilno*, 209k in 1939AD) and its
immediate hinterland (~549k inhabitants) to Lithuania, followed by full
Soviet occupation in June 1940AD (adding Swieciany/Svencionys and
Druskininkai from Belarus), the early-1941AD *Umsiedlung* of ~50 000
ethnic Germans and German-registered Lutherans, and the 14-18 June
1941AD NKVD deportation of 17 485 people to Altai Krai (Region 38),
Novosibirsk (Region 35) and the Komi ASSR (Region 18, *Sevzheldorlag*
and *Vorkutlag*)
[\[19\]](#ref-anusauskas1996teroras)[\[58\]](#ref-balkelis2005vilnius)[\[60\]](#ref-grunskis1996tremimai).

Between June and December 1941AD, Lithuania suffered one of the most
total and rapid genocides in Europe: *Einsatzkommando 3* (Karl Jager)
and Lithuanian collaborationist auxiliaries (*Ypatingasis burys* and TDA
battalions) murdered 190 000 to 195 000 Lithuanian and Vilnius Jews -
over 91-95% of Lithuania’s pre-war Jewish population - at Ponary
(*Paneriai*) outside Vilnius (~70 000 Jews shot alongside ~20 000 Polish
intelligentsia and Soviet POWs), the Ninth Fort at Kaunas (\>30 000
victims) and over two hundred provincial *shtetl* murder pits across
Siauliai, Panevezys, Ukmerge and Alytus
[\[23\]](#ref-arad2009holocaust)[\[58\]](#ref-balkelis2005vilnius). In
late 1944AD and January 1945AD, ~60 000 Lithuanians fled west, while
retreating Wehrmacht authorities evacuated virtually the entire civilian
population of Klaipeda (reducing the port from 48,5k in 1939AD to fewer
than 4k-6k civilians at liberation in January 1945AD). Between 1945AD
and 1946AD, under the Polish-Lithuanian evacuation agreements, 170 000
to 197 156 ethnic Poles were repatriated from Vilnius (110k in 1945AD)
and eastern Lithuania to Poland, radically transforming Vilnius from a
Polish-Jewish city into a Soviet Lithuanian-Russian administrative
capital [\[58\]](#ref-balkelis2005vilnius).

Unlike Estonia and Latvia, post-war Lithuania experienced minimal
all-Union Slavic industrial immigration before 1953AD because the
countryside was engulfed in the fiercest anti-Soviet guerrilla war in
the USSR (1944-1953AD): the Lithuanian Forest Brothers (*Misko broliai*,
united in 1949AD as the Movement of the Struggle for Freedom of
Lithuania, *LLKS*, under Jonas Zemaitis and Adolfas Ramanauskas)
mobilised ~30 000 to 50 000 active fighters over the conflict, losing 20
101 partisans killed in combat while killing ~13 000 MGB/MVD personnel,
*istrebiteli* militia and Soviet activists ([archived
PDF](papers/soviet_lenin_stalin_lithuania_deportations_kareniauskaite_2018.pdf))
[\[19\]](#ref-anusauskas1996teroras)[\[28\]](#ref-statiev2010borderlands)[\[61\]](#ref-kareniauskaite2018lithuania).
In retaliation, Moscow subjected Lithuania to four massive serial
deportations totalling 131 600 to 132 000 post-war deportees
(1945-1953AD, plus ~142 000 Gulag political prisoners). Early post-war
sweeps (1945-1947AD): ~12 000 partisans’ relatives and Lithuanian
Germans deported to Tajikistan, Komi (Region 18) and Sverdlovsk (Region
34).

Operation *Vesna* (“Spring”, 22-23 May 1948AD): The largest single
deportation in Lithuanian history, in which 30 000 MGB/MVD troops
deported 40 002 to 48 000 Lithuanians (11 345+ families) to timber camps
in Irkutsk Oblast (Region 40: Igirma, Bodaibo, Tayshet, Tulun),
Krasnoyarsk Krai (Region 40-41: Igarka, Yeniseysk) and the Buryat-Mongol
ASSR (Region 42) ([archived
PDF](papers/soviet_lenin_stalin_lithuania_vesna_rogers_2025.pdf);
[archived
PDF](papers/soviet_lenin_stalin_lithuania_deportees_juodenaite_2021.pdf))
[\[60\]](#ref-grunskis1996tremimai)[\[62\]](#ref-rogers2025lithuania)[\[63\]](#ref-juodenaite2021siberia).

Operation *Priboi* (25-28 March 1949AD): Deported 29 180 to 32 000
Lithuanians (8 765 families) to Irkutsk and Krasnoyarsk
[\[18\]](#ref-strods2002priboi)[\[60\]](#ref-grunskis1996tremimai).

Operation *Osen* (“Autumn”, 2-3 October 1951AD): Deported 16 150 to 20
357 remaining *kulak* family members to Krasnoyarsk and Tomsk Oblasts,
alongside smaller sweeps into the Kazakh SSR ([archived
PDF](papers/soviet_lenin_stalin_baltic_priboi_assymova_2026.pdf))
[\[60\]](#ref-grunskis1996tremimai)[\[64\]](#ref-assymova2026lithuanians).

Consequently, Lithuania (Region 3) fell from 3 030k in 1941AD to 2 460k
in 1945AD (-18,8%) and crept back to only 2 620k by 1953AD (-13,5% below
1941AD and even -0,9% below its 1926AD baseline!).

## Region 4: Moldavian SSR (2 040k in 1926AD -\> 2 470k in 1941AD -\> 1 940k in 1947AD Famine Trough -\> 2 520k in 1953AD)

Dedicated Region 4 (31 041 km2) unites central and northern Bessarabia
(Chisinau, Balti, Bender, Orhei, Soroca, Cahul - held by Greater Romania
from 1918AD to June 1940AD) with the western Transnistrian bank of the
Moldavian ASSR (MASSR, 1924-1940AD) (Tiraspol, Ribnita, Dubasari,
Slobozia), while assigning northern/eastern MASSR raions (Balta,
Birzula/Kotovsk, Ananiv) to Ukrainian Region 14 and southern Budjak
(Izmail, Cetatea Alba/Bilhorod-Dnistrovskyi) to Ukrainian Region 9
([archived
PDF](papers/soviet_lenin_stalin_moldavia_massr_bancila_2023.pdf))
[\[20\]](#ref-casu2014moldova)[\[42\]](#ref-bancila2023massr). During
the interwar period, left-bank MASSR (572,3k in 1926AD across all 11
original raions) endured dekulakisation, the 1932-1933AD famine
(~50k-67k excess deaths, prompting desperate peasant attempts to swim
the Dniester into Romania under OGPU border-guard fire, such as the
February 1932AD Olaneasca/Oloneshty crossing) and the 1937-1938AD NKVD
“Romanian Operation” (~5 500+ executions in Tiraspol), whereas
Romanian-ruled Bessarabia experienced high rural natural increase,
lifting Region 4 overall from 2 040k (1926AD) to 2 410k (1939AD)
[\[20\]](#ref-casu2014moldova)[\[42\]](#ref-bancila2023massr).

On 28 June 1940AD, following the Soviet ultimatum to Romania, the Red
Army occupied Bessarabia and on 2 August 1940AD proclaimed the Moldavian
SSR (Chisinau capital) [\[20\]](#ref-casu2014moldova). In autumn 1940AD,
93 468 Bessarabian Germans evacuated from southern/central German
colonies under the Nazi-Soviet *Umsiedlung* (Heim ins Reich), tens of
thousands of Romanian officials fled across the Prut, and on 12-13 June
1941AD the NKVD arrested or deported 22 648 to 29 839 inhabitants from
the Moldavian SSR (and ~6k-11k from Chernivtsi/Izmail in Region 9) to
Kazakhstan, Omsk, Novosibirsk and Komi
[\[4\]](#ref-polian2001against)[\[20\]](#ref-casu2014moldova).

During the 1941-1944AD Axis campaign, Ion Antonescu’s Romanian regime
reoccupied Bessarabia and administered *Transnistria* between the
Dniester and Southern Bug (Regions 4, 9, 10 and 14), perpetrating the
Bessarabian and Transnistrian Holocaust ([archived
PDF](papers/soviet_lenin_stalin_moldavia_holocaust_massr_dumitru_2013.pdf);
[archived
PDF](papers/soviet_lenin_stalin_moldavia_holocaust_massr_shapiro_2021.pdf);
[archived
PDF](papers/soviet_lenin_stalin_moldavia_holocaust_massr_matei_2022.pdf))
[\[23\]](#ref-arad2009holocaust)[\[24\]](#ref-shapiro2021transnistria)[\[65\]](#ref-dumitru2013bessarabia)[\[66\]](#ref-matei2022transnistria):
Romanian gendarmes, army units and German *Einsatzgruppe D* murdered
~45k-60k Jews directly in Bessarabian ghettos and transit camps
(Chisinau Ghetto, Marculesti, Vertujeni, Edinet) and force-marched the
survivors alongside Bukovinian Jews and 25 000 Romanian Roma into lethal
starvation and typhus camps across Transnistria (Bogdanovka, Domanevka,
Akhmetchetka, Vapniarka, Mogilev-Podolsky), where 200 000 to 280 000
local and deported Jews and ~11 000 Roma perished
[\[24\]](#ref-shapiro2021transnistria)[\[66\]](#ref-matei2022transnistria).
Heavy 1944AD combat (the Jassy-Kishinev Offensive, which left central
Chisinau \>70% destroyed, dropping from 112k in 1939AD to 65k in 1945AD)
reduced Region 4 from 2 470k (1941AD) to 2 080k in 1945AD.

Worse still followed in peace: between autumn 1946AD and summer 1947AD,
severe two-year drought across Bessarabia combined with relentless
MGB/party grain-procurement sweeps (khlebozagotovki enforced despite
near-total crop failure) triggered the 1946-1947AD Moldavian Famine
([archived
PDF](papers/soviet_lenin_stalin_moldavia_famine_casu_2020.pdf))
[\[20\]](#ref-casu2014moldova)[\[26\]](#ref-ellman2000famine1947)[\[27\]](#ref-casu2020famine1947)[\[67\]](#ref-zima1996golod).
Excess deaths in the Moldavian SSR reached 115 000 to 200 000 (5% to 8%
of the entire republic’s population - the highest per-capita mortality
anywhere in the post-war USSR), accompanied by \>390 000 cases of
alimentary dystrophy and hundreds of documented cases of cannibalism in
Cahul, Bender, Orhei and Balti districts, driving Region 4 down further
from 2 080k (1945AD) to its 1 940k famine trough in 1947AD
[\[26\]](#ref-ellman2000famine1947)[\[27\]](#ref-casu2020famine1947).
Two years later, on 6-7 July 1949AD, MGB forces executed Operation *Yug*
(“South”), deporting 35 796 Moldavian peasants (11 293 families labelled
*kulaks*, former Romanian landowners or Jehovah’s Witnesses - followed
by 2 617 Jehovah’s Witnesses in April 1951AD Operation *Sever*) in 30
freight echelons to Kurgan and Tyumen Oblasts (Region 35), Altai Krai
(Region 38), Irkutsk (Region 40) and the Kazakh SSR, breaking peasant
resistance to full collectivisation under First Secretary Nikolay Koval
and his 1950-1952AD successor Leonid Brezhnev ([archived
PDF](papers/soviet_lenin_stalin_moldavia_famine_yug_sandle_2016.pdf))
[\[20\]](#ref-casu2014moldova)[\[68\]](#ref-sandle2016moldavia). High
rural post-famine natality and Slavic administrative/industrial
in-migration to Chisinau (165k in 1953AD) and Tiraspol (48k) then
restored Region 4 to 2 520k by 1953AD.

## Region 5: Kaliningrad Oblast (1 120k in 1926AD -\> 1 220k in 1941AD -\> 180k in 1945AD -\> 540k in 1953AD)

Dedicated Region 5 (16 065 km2) isolates Northern East Prussia, awarded
to the USSR at the July-August 1945AD Potsdam Conference and
incorporated directly into the Russian SFSR on 7 April 1946AD as
*Konigsberg Oblast* (renamed Kaliningrad Oblast on 4 July 1946AD)
([archived PDF](papers/soviet_kaliningrad_repopulation_1945_2022_1.pdf);
[archived
PDF](papers/soviet_lenin_stalin_kaliningrad_baranova_hc_2023.pdf))
[\[25\]](#ref-kostyashov2022kaliningrad)[\[47\]](#ref-kostyashov2009sekretnaya)[\[69\]](#ref-kretinin2008kaliningrad)[\[70\]](#ref-baranova2023hc).
Under Weimar and Nazi rule, Northern East Prussia was a densely settled
German urban and agrarian province (1 120k in 1926AD -\> 1 210k in the
May 1939AD Reich census -\> 1 220k in 1941AD), led by the provincial
capital and Baltic port of Konigsberg (287k in 1926AD -\> 372,2k in
1939AD), Tilsit (*Sovetsk*) (50,8k -\> 59,1k), Insterburg
(*Chernyakhovsk*) (39,3k -\> 48,7k), Pillau (*Baltiysk*) (12,4k) and
Gumbinnen (*Gusev*) (24,6k).

During the Red Army’s East Prussian Offensive (January - April 1945AD),
the storming of Konigsberg (6-9 April 1945AD) and the Samland/Pillau
battles, ~900 000 to 1 000 000 German civilians fled west across the
Frische Haff and by sea from Pillau or perished in combat, leaving an
estimated 129 000 to 139 000 surviving German civilians (mostly women,
children and the elderly, alongside initial Red Army garrisons and
repatriated POWs, yielding 180k total inhabitants in late 1945AD - an
-85,2% demographic collapse from 1941AD, the steepest wartime drop of
all 45 regions!) ([archived
PDF](papers/soviet_lenin_stalin_kaliningrad_germans_brodersen_2012.pdf))
[\[47\]](#ref-kostyashov2009sekretnaya)[\[71\]](#ref-brodersen2012kaliningrad).
Between 1945AD and 1947AD, severe hunger, typhus, forced labour and lack
of rations reduced the surviving German civilian population to ~103 000
(with documented exhaustion mortality and isolated starvation crimes
during the 1946-1947AD winter)
[\[47\]](#ref-kostyashov2009sekretnaya)[\[69\]](#ref-kretinin2008kaliningrad).
Under Council of Ministers Resolution No. 3547-1169ss (11 October
1947AD), MVD authorities carried out the compulsory deportation of 102
125 to 102 405 remaining Germans between October 1947AD and November
1948AD (plus final stragglers in 1951AD) in 48 passenger/freight trains
to the Soviet Occupation Zone of Germany, effecting a 100% ethnic and
toponymic replacement of the province
[\[25\]](#ref-kostyashov2022kaliningrad)[\[71\]](#ref-brodersen2012kaliningrad).

To repopulate the shattered cities and flooded polder farmland, Stalin
signed Resolution No. 1298 (21 June 1946AD), launching a massive
organised resettlement campaign (*Pereselencheskoe upravlenie*) from
thirty war-ravaged oblasts and republics - above all Bryansk, Smolensk,
Kalinin, Oryol, Kursk, Voronezh, Tambov and Gorky Oblasts (Regions
21-23), the Mordovian and Chuvash ASSRs (Region 23) and Belarus (Regions
6-7) - alongside demobilised Baltic Fleet sailors at Baltiysk and
Kaliningrad ([archived
PDF](papers/soviet_kaliningrad_repopulation_1945_2023_2.pdf))
[\[25\]](#ref-kostyashov2022kaliningrad)[\[70\]](#ref-baranova2023hc)[\[72\]](#ref-baranova2023kaliningrad).
Despite high reverse migration (25-35% of early kolkhoz settlers left
due to ruined housing and unfamiliar drainage systems), steady state
recruitment lifted Region 5 from 180k (1945AD) to 340k (1947AD), 475k
(1950AD) and 540k in 1953AD (Kaliningrad city reaching 175k, Sovetsk
28k, Chernyakhovsk 26k), still -55,7% below its 1941AD pre-war level.

# Border Cleansings, the 1937AD Korean Exile, Karelia, and the Volga-Ural ASSRs

## Pre-War Border Cleansings (1935-1938AD), the 1937AD Korean Deportation (Region 45), and the Great Terror

Between 1935AD and 1938AD, Soviet demographic policy shifted from
class-based dekulakisation to pre-emptive ethnic cleansing of western
and eastern border zones
[\[4\]](#ref-polian2001against)[\[12\]](#ref-martin2001affirmative)[\[22\]](#ref-pohl1999ethnic).
Western Border-Zone Expulsions of Poles, Germans and Ingrian Finns
(1935-1936AD): In February-March 1935AD and January-September 1936AD,
the NKVD deported ~42 000 Polish and German families (~195 000 persons,
including the liquidation of the Marchlewski Polish National Raion and
Pulin German National Raion in Zhytomyr/Volhynia, Region 10, and border
raions of Region 7 in Belarus) to northern Kazakhstan and Siberia;
simultaneously, in 1935-1936AD, ~27 000 to 45 000 Ingrian Finns
(*Inkerilaiset*) were forcibly cleared from the Estonian and Finnish
border raions of Leningrad Oblast (Region 17) and Karelia (Region 16) to
Vologda, Western Siberia and Tajikistan
[\[4\]](#ref-polian2001against)[\[12\]](#ref-martin2001affirmative).

The September-October 1937AD Total Deportation of 171 781 Soviet Koreans
from Primorye (Region 45): Under Sovnarkom and Central Committee
Resolution No. 1428-326ss of 21 August 1937AD (signed by Stalin and
Molotov), the entire ethnic Korean population (*Koryo-saram*) of the
Soviet Far East (Region 45: Posyet, Vladivostok, Spassk,
Ussuriysk/Voroshilov and Khabarovsk border districts) was subjected to
the first total nationality deportation in Soviet history
[\[4\]](#ref-polian2001against)[\[22\]](#ref-pohl1999ethnic). Within
eight weeks (September-October 1937AD), 124 echelons transported 36 442
Korean families (171 781 persons) across 6 000 kilometres to Uzbekistan
(76 525) and Kazakhstan (95 256), where ~25k to 40k died of exposure and
typhus during 1937-1938AD, while vacated Korean collective farms in
Region 45 were repopulated by demobilised Red Army men and
Russian/Ukrainian settlers [\[4\]](#ref-polian2001against).

The 1937-1938AD Great Terror and NKVD National Operations: Under NKVD
Order No. 00447 (“kulak operation”) and the “national operations”
(Polish Order No. 00485, German Order No. 00439, Romanian, Harbinite,
Latvian, Estonian, Finnish, Greek and Korean directives), ~1,57M people
were arrested and 681 692 were executed in 1937-1938AD, striking
disproportionately at borderland minorities in Regions 4, 7, 10, 14, 16,
17 and 45 and political exiles inside the Gulag
[\[12\]](#ref-martin2001affirmative)[\[13\]](#ref-khlevniuk2004history).

## Karelia (Region 16): Belomorcanal, “Karelian Fever”, the 1940/1944 Evacuation of 422 000 Finnish Karelians, and the Karelo-Finnish SSR

Region 16 (337 469 km2) exemplifies the intersection of Gulag
mega-projects, ethnic purges, border annexations and complete population
replacement
[\[14\]](#ref-kustyshev2022kolagulag)[\[73\]](#ref-baron2007karelia).
The White Sea-Baltic Canal (*Belomorcanal / BBK*, 1931-1933AD) and Kola
Industrialisation: Constructed in twenty months (November 1931AD -
August 1933AD) across 227 km from Lake Onega (Povenets) to the White Sea
(Soroka/Belomorsk) by an average contingent of 107 900 *Belbaltlag*
prisoners (with ~170k passing through the camp and ~12 000 to 25 000
officially recorded camp deaths), the canal was managed from
Medvezhyegorsk (Region 16)
[\[16\]](#ref-applebaum2003gulag)[\[73\]](#ref-baron2007karelia).
*Belbaltlag* and *Segezhlag* subsequently built pulp-and-paper,
aluminium and nickel complexes at Segezha, Nadvoitsy and Monchegorsk
(*Severonikel* on the Kola Peninsula), while Murmansk expanded from 8
777 (1926AD) to 118 690 (1939AD), driving Region 16 from 860k (1926AD)
to 1 390k (1939AD, including pre-war Finnish Viipuri/Vyborg province)
([archived PDF](papers/soviet_gulag_demography_2022_1.pdf))
[\[14\]](#ref-kustyshev2022kolagulag).

“Karelian Fever” (1930-1935AD) and the 1937-1938AD NKVD Finnish
Operation: Recruited by Karelian ASSR leader Edvard Gylling to modernise
Karelian forestry, ~6 000 to 6 500 North American Finns (from the United
States and Canada) and ~8 000 Red Finns from Finland immigrated to
Petrozavodsk, Kondopoga and Uroszero in the early 1930s. During the
1937-1938AD Great Terror, the NKVD executed Gylling, abolished Finnish
national status in Karelia, and arrested or shot over 9 000 ethnic Finns
in Karelia (~38% of all Great Terror victims in the republic despite
Finns forming \<3% of the population) [\[73\]](#ref-baron2007karelia).

The 1939-1940AD Winter War, the 1940/1944AD Evacuation of ~422 000
Finnish Karelians, and the Karelo-Finnish SSR (1940-1956AD): Following
the Moscow Peace Treaty (12 March 1940AD), the USSR annexed the Karelian
Isthmus (Viipuri / Vyborg), Ladoga Karelia (Sortavala,
Kakisalmi/Priozersk, Suojarvi) and Salla (~35 000 km2) and elevated
Karelia on 31 March 1940AD into the 16th Union Republic: the
Karelo-Finnish SSR
[\[4\]](#ref-polian2001against)[\[73\]](#ref-baron2007karelia).
Virtually the entire indigenous Finnish Karelian population (~422 000
people, \>99% of the ceded territory’s inhabitants) evacuated across the
new border into Finland in March 1940AD rather than remain under Soviet
rule. After ~260 000 Karelians returned during the 1941-1944AD
Continuation War, they evacuated a second and final time (~407 000 total
evacuees) upon the September 1944AD Moscow Armistice, leaving Vyborg and
Sortavala empty of their pre-war population. Consequently, Region 16
fell from 1 390k (1939AD) to 1 120k (1941AD) and 680k (1945AD, -39,3%
below 1941AD), before organised kolkhoz and timber resettlement from
Belarus, Vologda, Central Russia and Ukraine restored it to 1 120k by
1953AD [\[4\]](#ref-polian2001against).

## Komi ASSR (Region 18), Volga-Kama ASSRs (Regions 23-24), and the Great Ural Arsenal (Region 34)

Across the northern and Volga-Ural autonomous republics of the RSFSR,
Stalinist industrialisation and forced labour produced dramatic
demographic transformations (Figure and Table ). Region 18 (Komi ASSR &
Nenets National Okrug: Vorkutlag, Intalag, Ukhtpechlag, Sevzheldorlag):
Elevated from the Komi (Zyryan) Autonomous Oblast to the Komi ASSR under
the 5 December 1936AD Stalin Constitution, Region 18 more than tripled
from 240k in 1926AD to 430k in 1939AD, 560k in 1945AD and 750k in 1953AD
(+212,5% over 1926AD - the only European USSR region to expand
continuously throughout the 1941-1945AD war!). Following the 1929AD OGPU
Ukhta expedition, *Ukhtpechlag* (1932-1938AD) was split into *Vorkutlag*
(mining Pechora coking coal at 67,5°N to replace occupied Donbas coal
during the war), *Intalag* (Inta energy coal), *Ukhtizhemlag* (Ukhta oil
and radium/gas) and *Sevzheldorlag* (which built the 1 191 km
Kotlas-Pechora-Vorkuta North Pechora Railway, completed in December
1941AD at the cost of \>30k prisoner lives)
[\[13\]](#ref-khlevniuk2004history)[\[16\]](#ref-applebaum2003gulag). By
1953AD, Vorkuta (65k free residents plus 73k inmates in *Vorkutlag* and
Special Camp No. 6 *Rechlag*, site of the July-August 1953AD Vorkuta
Uprising, where Baltic and Western Ukrainian political prisoners played
a leading role), Ukhta (26k), Inta (24k) and Pechora (22k) had reduced
ethnic Komi to a minority (~30%) inside their own republic.

Region 23 (Chuvash, Mari, Mordovian & Udmurt ASSRs + Gorky & Kirov):
Grew from 6 770k (1926AD) to 7 280k (1941AD) and 6 950k (1953AD). In the
Mordovian ASSR (1934AD), rural 1933AD famine and out-migration were
offset by the massive *Temlag / Dubravlag* camp complex at Yavas and
Potma (~50k+ prisoners, including Special Camp No. 3 for political
prisoners from 1948AD); the Udmurt ASSR (1934AD) centred on Izhevsk (63k
in 1926AD -\> 176k in 1939AD -\> 245k in 1953AD), which produced over
11,1M rifles and carbines during 1941-1945AD; Gorky (*GAZ* automotive
plant) jumped from 222k (1926AD) to 644k (1939AD) and 830k (1953AD); and
northern Kirov Oblast hosted *Vyatlag* (~30k+ timber prisoners,
including 1941AD Latvian and Estonian deportees) ([archived
PDF](papers/soviet_wwii_losses_demography_2009_2.pdf))
[\[13\]](#ref-khlevniuk2004history)[\[74\]](#ref-skvortsova2009mordovia).

Region 24 (Tatar ASSR & Ulyanovsk-Penza) and Region 34 (Great Ural
Arsenal & Bashkir ASSR): In the Tatar ASSR (Kazan: 179k in 1926AD -\>
398k in 1939AD -\> 560k in 1953AD) and Bashkir ASSR (Ufa: 99k -\> 250k
-\> 380k; Ishimbai, Tuymazy and Salavat/Novostroika), the discovery of
the “Second Baku” (*Vtoroy Baku*) Volga-Ural oilfields (1932-1944AD) and
the 1941-1942AD evacuation of 667+ defence factories transformed Region
34 from 9 100k (1926AD) to 11 900k (1941AD), 13 500k (1943AD) and 14
390k (1953AD, +58,1% over 1926AD), powered by ~350k+ mobilised Soviet
German and minority *Trudarmiya* (“Labour Army”) conscripts in
Chelyabinsk (*Bakallag*/*Chelyabmetallurgstroy*), *Bogoslovlag*
(Karpinsk/Krasnoturyinsk aluminium), *Tagillag* and Solikamsk
(*Usollag*) ([archived
PDF](papers/soviet_wwii_evacuation_urals_siberia_2006_2.pdf))
[\[75\]](#ref-kruglikov2006sverdlovsk)[\[76\]](#ref-potemkina2002evakuatsiya).

# Wartime Ethnic Deportations and ASSR Liquidations (Regions 15, 26, 28, 30, 31, 32, and 33)

Between August 1941AD and June 1944AD, Stalin, Beria and the State
Defence Committee (*GKO*) carried out seven total ethnic deportations
(*totalnye deportatsii “nakazannykh narodov”*), abolishing five
autonomous republics and autonomous oblasts across the Lower Volga,
Crimea and the North Caucasus and deporting their entire indigenous
populations in sealed cattle cars to special settlements
(*spetsposeleniya*) in Kazakhstan, Central Asia and Siberia ([archived
PDF](papers/soviet_ethnic_deportations_1941_1944_2005_1.pdf); [archived
PDF](papers/soviet_ethnic_deportations_1941_1944_2020_2.pdf))
[\[4\]](#ref-polian2001against)[\[6\]](#ref-zemskov2003spetsposelentsy)[\[21\]](#ref-bugai1995deportatsiya)[\[22\]](#ref-pohl1999ethnic)[\[77\]](#ref-shalak2005eastsiberia)[\[78\]](#ref-shnayder2020northcaucasus).
Our 45-region partition explicitly isolates each liquidated autonomous
unit (Regions 15, 26, 28, 30, 31, 32 and 33) (Figure , and Table ).

Region 26: Volga German ASSR (Liquidated August-September 1941AD):

Under the Supreme Soviet Presidium Decree of 28 August 1941AD (“On the
Resettlement of the Germans Living in the Volga Districts”), the Volga
German ASSR (*ASSR Nemtsev Povolzhya*, founded 1918/1924AD with its
capital at Engels/Pokrovsk and cantonal centres at Marxstadt/Marks,
Balzer/Krasnoarmeysk, Seelmann/Rovnoe, Kukkus and Pallasovka) was
abolished on 7 September 1941AD, and its 22 cantons were partitioned
between Saratov Oblast (15 cantons) and Stalingrad Oblast (7 cantons)
[\[4\]](#ref-polian2001against)[\[43\]](#ref-german2000nemtsy).

Between 3 and 20 September 1941AD, NKVD troops loaded 438 700 Volga
Germans (virtually the entire ethnic German population of Region 26,
alongside ~42k Germans from neighbouring Saratov and Stalingrad raions)
into 188 freight echelons bound for Altai Krai (Region 38, ~95k),
Novosibirsk and Omsk Oblasts (Region 35, ~195k), Krasnoyarsk Krai
(Region 40, ~75k) and the Kazakh SSR
[\[43\]](#ref-german2000nemtsy)[\[77\]](#ref-shalak2005eastsiberia).
Combined with the 1941-1942AD deportation of \>450k Soviet Germans from
Ukraine, Crimea, Transcaucasia and Leningrad (~950k wartime deportees)
and the conscription of ~316k adult German men and women into NKVD
*Trudarmiya* timber and construction camps in Regions 18, 34 and 37,
total wartime German forced displacement reached ~1,2M
[\[6\]](#ref-zemskov2003spetsposelentsy)[\[22\]](#ref-pohl1999ethnic).

As a direct result, Region 26 plummeted by -66,1% from 620k in June
1941AD to 210k in 1943AD and 250k in 1945AD (-59,7%), recovering only to
340k by 1953AD (-45,2% below 1941AD) as evacuated and resettled Russian
and Ukrainian collective farmers only partially refilled the depopulated
German steppes.

Region 30: Stavropol Krai, Karachay AO (Liquidated November 1943AD) &
Cherkess AO:

Following the Supreme Soviet Decree of 12 October 1943AD, on 2-5
November 1943AD NKVD units deported 14 774 families (69 267 persons) of
the Karachay Autonomous Oblast (capital Mikoyan-Shakhar, renamed
Klukhori/Karachaevsk) in 34 echelons to the Kazakh (45 529) and Kirghiz
(23 300) SSRs, where ~15k to 19k (~22%, including over 10k children)
perished from typhus and malnutrition during 1943-1945AD
[\[21\]](#ref-bugai1995deportatsiya)[\[78\]](#ref-shnayder2020northcaucasus).

The Karachay AO was abolished: its northern districts were absorbed into
Stavropol Krai and the Cherkess AO, while its southern high-mountain
Caucasus districts (Mikoyan-Shakhar and Uchkulan) were annexed to the
Georgian SSR as Klukhorsky Raion until 1955AD
[\[79\]](#ref-tsutsiev2014atlas). Combined with wartime losses across
Stavropol, Region 30 fell by -16,7% from 1 740k (1941AD) to 1 450k
(1945AD).

Region 28: Kalmyk ASSR (Liquidated December 1943AD, Operation *Ulusy*):

Under the Supreme Soviet Decree of 27 December 1943AD, NKVD Chief Beria
supervised Operation *Ulusy* (28-31 December 1943AD), in which 10 000
NKVD troops deported 26 359 Kalmyk families (93 139 persons, followed by
~6k Kalmyks from Rostov and Stalingrad oblasts and demobilised Red Army
soldiers sent to the *Shirokovlag* hydro-camp) in 46 unheated winter
cattle-car echelons to Altai Krai (Region 38), Omsk, Tyumen and
Novosibirsk Oblasts (Regions 35-36) and Krasnoyarsk Krai (Regions
40-41), with ~3 500 Kalmyks sent in 1944AD to Far Eastern fishery
brigades on Sakhalin and the Tatar Strait (Region 45)
[\[4\]](#ref-polian2001against)[\[21\]](#ref-bugai1995deportatsiya)[\[77\]](#ref-shalak2005eastsiberia).
Over 16 500 Kalmyks (\>17% of the deported nation) died in transit and
during 1944-1945AD.

The Kalmyk ASSR was abolished, its capital Elista was renamed Stepnoy,
and its territory was partitioned among Astrakhan, Stalingrad and Rostov
Oblasts and Stavropol Krai, causing Region 28 to plunge by -64,9% from
185k (1941AD) to 65k (1945AD), reaching only 100k by 1953AD (-45,9%
below 1941AD).

Region 32: Chechen-Ingush ASSR -\> Grozny Oblast (Liquidated
February-March 1944AD, Operation *Chechevitsa*):

Under GKO Resolution No. 5073ss (31 January 1944AD), Beria, Kobulov,
Serov and 119 000 NKVD/SMERSH troops launched Operation *Chechevitsa*
(“Lentil”) at dawn on 23 February 1944AD (Red Army Day)
[\[4\]](#ref-polian2001against)[\[21\]](#ref-bugai1995deportatsiya)[\[22\]](#ref-pohl1999ethnic).
Using ~12 000 Lend-Lease Studebaker trucks to clear snowbound mountain
*auls* and 180 freight echelons (14 200 wagons), the NKVD deported 478
479 Vainakhs (387 229 Chechens and 91 250 Ingush) within seven days;
including Chechens and Ingush deported from other republics and
demobilised from the Red Army, the total registered contingent reached
493 269 to 496 460
[\[6\]](#ref-zemskov2003spetsposelentsy)[\[21\]](#ref-bugai1995deportatsiya).
In the high-mountain village of Khaibakh (Galanchozhsky Raion), on 27
February 1944AD, NKVD officers under M. Gveshiani locked ~650 to 700
women, children and elderly residents unable to march through deep snow
inside a collective-farm stable and burned them alive
[\[4\]](#ref-polian2001against).

Deported to the Kazakh SSR (~411k) and Kirghiz SSR (~85k), the Chechens
and Ingush suffered catastrophic typhus, exposure and starvation
mortality: NKVD special-settlement records document 123 000 to 144 704
deaths between 1944AD and 1948AD (23,7% to 25,0% of the entire Chechen
and Ingush nations)
[\[6\]](#ref-zemskov2003spetsposelentsy)[\[78\]](#ref-shnayder2020northcaucasus).

On 7 March 1944AD, the Chechen-Ingush ASSR was abolished and dismembered
into four pieces: (a) the central and northern plains around Grozny and
the Sunzha/Terek valleys became Grozny Oblast of the RSFSR; (b)
Prigorodny Raion (East Vladikavkaz), Nazran, Malgobek and Achkhoy-Martan
were transferred to the North Ossetian ASSR (Region 31, with
Vladikavkaz/Ordzhonikidze renamed Dzaudzhikau); (c) the eastern mountain
districts (Vedeno, Nozhay-Yurt, Sayasan, Kurchaloy, Cheberloy, Sharoy)
were annexed to the Dagestan ASSR (Region 33); and (d) the southern
Itum-Kale gorge was annexed to the Georgian SSR (Akhalkhevi Raion)
[\[79\]](#ref-tsutsiev2014atlas). Despite organised Slavic resettlement
into the Grozny oilfields and Sunzha valley, Region 32 collapsed by
-57,0% from 790k (1941AD) to 340k (1945AD), reaching only 490k in 1953AD
(-38,0% below 1941AD).

Region 31: Kabardino-Balkar ASSR (March 1944AD Balkar Deportation) &
North Ossetian ASSR:

On 8-9 March 1944AD, immediately after Operation *Chechevitsa*, 19 000
NKVD troops deported the entire Balkar population (37 713 persons) from
the high-mountain Baksan, Chegem, Cherek and Khulam-Bezengi gorges in 14
echelons to Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan (~22% died in 1944-1948AD)
[\[21\]](#ref-bugai1995deportatsiya)[\[78\]](#ref-shnayder2020northcaucasus).

By Decree of 8 April 1944AD, the republic was renamed the Kabardian ASSR
(capital Nalchik), and its upper Baksan and Mount Elbrus territories
were ceded to the Georgian SSR (Upper Svanetia). Meanwhile, the North
Ossetian ASSR absorbed Ingush Prigorodny Raion and was resettled with
~45k Ossetians from both North Ossetia and South Ossetia/Georgia,
resulting in Region 31 declining by -18,3% from 930k (1941AD) to 760k
(1945AD) before recovering to 930k by 1953AD
[\[79\]](#ref-tsutsiev2014atlas).

Region 33: Dagestan ASSR (February-May 1944AD Aukhovsky Chechen
Deportation & Forced Mountain Resettlements):

During Operation *Chechevitsa* (23-29 February 1944AD), the NKVD also
deported the entire Aukhovsky (Akkin) Chechen population (14 542
persons) from Aukhovsky Raion (founded October 1943AD in northwestern
Dagestan around Khasavyurt) to Kazakhstan ([archived
PDF](papers/soviet_1937_1939_census_2023_1.pdf))
[\[34\]](#ref-amirkhanova2023dagestan)[\[79\]](#ref-tsutsiev2014atlas).

To erase Chechen presence and secure the annexed eastern Chechen
mountain districts (Vedeno, Nozhay-Yurt, Sayasan, Kurchaloy), Sovnarkom
Resolution No. 255-74ss (11 March 1944AD) ordered the compulsory
internal resettlement of ~55 000 to 65 000 Dagestani highlanders:
Aukhovsky Raion was renamed Novolaksky Raion and forcibly settled with
~6 000 to 8 000 mountain Laks from Kuli and Laksky raions (Kazi-Kumukh),
while ~50 000 Avars, Andi-Tsez peoples and Dargins were marched from
central Dagestan into the vacated Chechen villages (renamed Andalal,
Rikvani, Ritlyab and Shuragatl). Lacking shelter, tools or immunity to
lowland malaria and typhus, up to 15-20% of the forcibly resettled
Dagestani mountaineers perished between 1944AD and 1947AD, contributing
(alongside heavy wartime military losses) to Region 33 falling by -20,9%
from 860k (1941AD) to 680k (1945AD, 760k in 1953AD)
[\[34\]](#ref-amirkhanova2023dagestan).

Region 15: Crimean ASSR -\> Crimean Oblast (May-June 1944AD Crimean
Tatar & Minority Deportations):

Following the liberation of Sevastopol, GKO Resolution No. 5859ss (11
May 1944AD) ordered the total deportation of the Crimean Tatars
[\[4\]](#ref-polian2001against)[\[21\]](#ref-bugai1995deportatsiya). On
18-20 May 1944AD, 32 000 NKVD troops deported 191 044 Crimean Tatars (47
000 families, including ~6k deported from the Kerch peninsula in April)
in 67 echelons to the Uzbek SSR (151 604), the Mari ASSR (Region 23,
~8,5k), Molotov and Sverdlovsk Oblasts (Region 34, ~20k) and
Kostroma/Gorky timber camps, where ~27k to 44k (15-22%) died during
1944-1948AD
[\[6\]](#ref-zemskov2003spetsposelentsy)[\[22\]](#ref-pohl1999ethnic).

Five weeks later, under GKO Resolution No. 5984ss (2 June 1944AD), on
24-27 June 1944AD the NKVD deported ~41 854 additional Crimean
minorities: 15 040 Crimean Greeks, 12 422 Bulgarians, 9 621 Armenians
and ~4 770 Germans, Italians and foreign nationals (following the August
1941AD deportation of ~53 000 Crimean Germans). Combined with the
250-day Siege of Sevastopol (1941-1942AD) and the Nazi murder of ~40 000
Crimean Jews and Krymchaks, Region 15 collapsed by -49,2% from 1 180k
(1941AD) to 600k (1945AD). On 30 June 1945AD, the Crimean ASSR was
downgraded to Crimean Oblast, Tatar, Greek and German toponyms
(*Karasubazar* -\> Belogorsk) were Russified, and organised kolkhoz
resettlement brought \>350 000 Russian and Ukrainian settlers from
Voronezh, Kursk, Oryol, Bryansk, Rostov, Gorky and Ukraine, raising
Region 15 to 980k by 1953AD (still -16,9% below 1941AD)
[\[4\]](#ref-polian2001against).

# Siberian Republics, Polar Gulag, and the Great Patriotic War (1941AD - 1953AD)

## Disaggregated Siberian Regions (Regions 35-45): Trans-Siberian Industrial Spine vs Special-Settlement Taiga, Polar Gulag, and Indigenous Republics

A crucial feature of our 45-region partition is the disaggregation of
Siberia and the Far East into eleven distinct regions (Regions 35-45, 12
853 253 km2) that separate the rapidly urbanising Trans-Siberian
industrial and evacuation corridor (Regions 35, 37, 38, 40, 45) from the
northern special-settlement and polar Gulag zones (Regions 36, 41, 44)
and the indigenous Siberian autonomous republics and oblasts (Regions
39, 42, 43) (Table ).

Region 43: Yakut ASSR (Aldan Gold, the 1942AD Churapcha Tragedy, and
Arctic Lena Delta Exiles):

Spanning 3,06M km2, the Yakut ASSR grew from 285k in 1926AD to 415k in
1939AD and 425k in 1941AD as the Aldan (Nezametny) and Allakh-Yun
(*Dzhugdzhurlag*) goldfields drew tens of thousands of Russian miners
and special settlers, reducing the indigenous Sakha (Yakut) share of the
republic’s population from 81,6% (1926AD) to 56,5% (1939AD)
[\[80\]](#ref-antonov2018churapcha).

During the Great Patriotic War, Region 43 contracted from 425k (1941AD)
to 400k (1943-1945AD) due to heavy military conscription (~62k
mobilised), rural famine and the August-September 1942AD Churapcha
Tragedy (*Churapchinskaya tragediya*)
[\[80\]](#ref-antonov2018churapcha): under the Yakut VKP(b) Obkom
resolution of 11 August 1942AD (signed by I. Vinokurov), 4 988 Sakha
collective farmers (1 781 households from 41 drought-stricken *alaas*
cattle-breeding kolkhozes in Churapcha Ulus) were forcibly marched to
the Lena River and shipped by barge 1 500+ km north of the Arctic Circle
to unprepared fishing camps in Kobyaysky (2 023), Zhigansky (1 178) and
Bulunsky (1 787) uluses to supply fish for the front. Stripped of
livestock and dumped onto freezing tundra without nets, winter housing
or rations, 1 748 Churapcha deportees (\>35% of the resettled
population) perished from starvation and scurvy between 1942AD and
1944AD before the survivors were permitted to return in 1944-1946AD
[\[80\]](#ref-antonov2018churapcha).

Concurrently, in 1942AD, the NKVD deported ~12 000 Leningrad Ingrian
Finns (evacuated across Lake Ladoga from Region 17), Volga Germans
(Region 26) and June 1941AD Lithuanian deportees (Region 3) to the
treeless islands of the Arctic Lena Delta (Trofimovsk, Bykov Mys,
Tit-Ary, Stolbovoy at 72-73°N), where ice-block barracks and scurvy
killed over 40% of the exiles during the winter of 1942-1943AD
[\[4\]](#ref-polian2001against)[\[80\]](#ref-antonov2018churapcha).
Post-war expansion of *Yanastroy*/*Dalstroy* tin and gold camps at
Ust-Nera (Oymyakon) and Khandyga raised Region 43 to 470k by 1953AD.

Region 42: Buryat-Mongol ASSR (1937AD Territorial Dismemberment) &
Chita/Transbaikalia (*Bamlag*):

In the Buryat-Mongol ASSR (founded 1923AD, capital Verkhneudinsk,
renamed Ulan-Ude in 1934AD), brutal cattle collectivisation and
anti-Buddhist terror (1929-1932AD, which destroyed \>60% of the
republic’s livestock and closed all 44 *datsans*) provoked armed
uprisings in Mukhorshibir, Bichura and Zakamensk and the flight of ~35
000 Buryats and Transbaikal Old Believers (*Semeiskie*) across the
border into Mongolia and Manchuria.

At the height of the Great Terror, on 26 September 1937AD, the Central
Executive Committee of the USSR dismembered the Buryat-Mongol ASSR,
detaching six western Buryat *aimaks* (Alarsky, Bokhansky,
Ekhirit-Bulagatsky, Nukutsky) into Irkutsk Oblast (Region 40) as the
Ust-Orda Buryat National Okrug and two eastern *aimaks* (Aginsky,
Ulan-Ononsky) into Chita Oblast as the Agin-Buryat National Okrug, while
NKVD officers executed First Secretary Mikhey Yerbanov and the Buryat
intellectual elite on charges of “Pan-Mongolist Japanese espionage”
[\[12\]](#ref-martin2001affirmative).

Simultaneously, Five-Year-Plan construction of the giant Ulan-Ude
Locomotive and Wagon Works (*UULVRZ*) and Aircraft Plant No. 99
propelled Ulan-Ude from 28 918 (1926AD) to 125 690 (1939AD) and 158 000
(1953AD), while NKVD *Dzhidlag* prisoners (joined in 1948AD by
Lithuanian Operation *Vesna* timber exiles) mined strategic tungsten and
molybdenum at Gorodok (Zakamensk) and *Bamlag*/*Zabaikallag* prisoners
expanded the Transbaikal railway and gold/tin mines across Chita Oblast,
carrying Region 42 from 1 050k (1926AD) to 1 970k (1953AD, +87,6%).

Region 39: Oyrot/Gorno-Altai AO, Khakass AO (Kulak Mines), Minusinsk &
Tuva AO (October 1944AD Annexation):

Across the Altai-Sayan autonomous belt (Region 39: 490k in 1926AD -\>
960k in 1953AD, +95,9%), the Khakass Autonomous Oblast (established 20
October 1930AD, capital Abakan: 3,1k in 1926AD -\> 36,6k in 1939AD -\>
52k in 1953AD) became a major reception zone for dekulakised peasants
(1930-1933AD), Volga Germans (1941AD), Kalmyks (1944AD) and
Baltic/Western Ukrainian deportees (1944-1949AD) in the Chernogorsk coal
basin and *Balyksinsk*/*Sarala* goldfields, reducing ethnic Khakass from
53,0% (1926AD) to 16,8% (1939AD) of their oblast’s population.

In the Oyrot Autonomous Oblast (founded 1922AD), collectivisation and
the 1933AD famine triggered flight into Xinjiang; on 7 January 1948AD,
during late Stalinist ideological campaigns against
“bourgeois-nationalist Oyrot epics”, the oblast was renamed Gorno-Altai
Autonomous Oblast and its capital *Oyrot-Tura* became Gorno-Altaysk.

On 11 October 1944AD, the formerly independent Tuvan People’s Republic
(*Tannu-Tuva*, 170 500 km2, capital Kyzyl, population ~95 000, ~81%
Tuvan nomadic herders plus Russian settlers) was secretly annexed by
Presidium Decree as the Tuva Autonomous Oblast of the RSFSR, followed by
forced collectivisation of Tuvan *arats* during 1948-1953AD
[\[4\]](#ref-polian2001against).

Regions 35, 37, 38, 40, 41, 44 and 45: Trans-Siberian Industrial Hubs,
Kuzbass, Altai Deportees, Polar Norillag, Dalstroy Kolyma and Southern
Far East:

Region 35 (West Siberian Trans-Siberian Spine: Tyumen, Kurgan, Omsk,
Novosibirsk, Tomsk) rose from 3 880k (1926AD) to 5 080k (1941AD), surged
to 5 880k (1943AD) as 322+ evacuated defence and optical/aviation plants
settled in Novosibirsk (120k -\> 404k -\> 710k) and Omsk (162k -\> 289k
-\> 490k), absorbed tens of thousands of Estonian, Latvian and Moldavian
*Priboi*/*Yug* deportees in 1949AD across Omsk, Novosibirsk, Tomsk,
Kurgan and Tyumen, and reached 6 340k in 1953AD (+63,4%) ([archived
PDF](papers/soviet_1937_1939_census_2013_2.pdf); [archived
PDF](papers/soviet_famine_1946_1947_2013_2.pdf))
[\[18\]](#ref-strods2002priboi)[\[35\]](#ref-dmitrienko2013tomsk1939)[\[81\]](#ref-laperdin2013omsk1947).

Region 37 (Kuzbass Coal-Steel Combine & Siblag: Kemerovo Oblast,
detached 26 January 1943AD) more than tripled from 750k (1926AD) to 1
820k (1941AD), 2 150k (1943AD) and 2 520k (1953AD, +236,0%), anchored by
Stalinsk/Novokuznetsk (3,9k -\> 166k -\> 315k), Prokopyevsk (11k -\>
107k -\> 240k) and Kemerovo (22k -\> 133k -\> 235k), while assimilating
~78k *Siblag* prisoners, \>100k Volga German *Trudarmiya* coal miners
and the liquidated Mountain Shoria (*Gornaya Shoriya*) National Raion
(abolished 1939AD, where Shor iron-smelters fell from \>50% to \<6%).

Region 38 (Altai Krai Deportee & Grain Hub: Barnaul, Rubtsovsk, Biysk,
Slavgorod) grew from 2 280k (1926AD) to 2 680k (1941AD), jumped to 3
050k (1943AD) with the arrival of ~95k deported Volga Germans, ~22k
Kalmyks, 1941AD Lithuanian and 1949AD Moldavian exiles, Leningrad
evacuees and the evacuated Kharkiv Tractor Plant at Rubtsovsk (*Altai
Tractor Plant*), and reached 3 240k in 1953AD (+42,1%) ([archived
PDF](papers/soviet_famine_1946_1947_2012_1.pdf))
[\[82\]](#ref-laperdin2012westsiberia1947).

Region 40 (East Siberian Trans-Siberian Corridor: Krasnoyarsk *Kraslag*
& Irkutsk-Tayshet *Ozerlag*) more than doubled from 1 920k (1926AD) to 3
950k (1953AD, +105,7%), receiving the largest concentrations of
1948-1951AD Lithuanian, Estonian and Western Ukrainian deportees
alongside MVD Special Camp No. 7 (*Ozerlag* at Tayshet, ~37k political
prisoners) and *Angarstroy* (115k prisoners) building the western
Baikal-Amur Mainline and early Angarsk ([archived
PDF](papers/soviet_ethnic_deportations_1941_1944_2005_1.pdf); [archived
PDF](papers/soviet_lenin_stalin_lithuania_deportees_juodenaite_2021.pdf))
[\[63\]](#ref-juodenaite2021siberia)[\[77\]](#ref-shalak2005eastsiberia).

Region 41 (Yenisei-Taymyr Polar Gulag & Tundra: Norillag, Igarka, Taymyr
& Evenkia) surged by +354,5% from 110k (1926AD) to 500k (1953AD).
Centred on Norilsk (69,3°N), *Norillag* (1935-1956AD, 72 500 inmates
plus Special Camp No. 2 *Gorlag*, where Baltic and Ukrainian political
prisoners spearheaded the May-August 1953AD Norilsk Uprising) produced
over 35% of Soviet nickel alongside copper, cobalt and platinum, while
~25k kulak and Lithuanian/Finnish exiles worked at Igarka and in the
Taymyr (Dolgan-Nenets) and Evenki National Okrugs (founded December
1930AD)
[\[13\]](#ref-khlevniuk2004history)[\[16\]](#ref-applebaum2003gulag).

Region 44 (Dalstroy Extreme Northeast Gulag Complex: Kolyma-Magadan
*Sevvostlag*, Chukotka & Kamchatka) expanded twelvefold (+1 100,0%!)
from 35k (1926AD) to 255k (1941AD) and 420k (1953AD, detached as Magadan
Oblast in December 1953AD). Established in November 1931AD under Eduard
Berzin, the NKVD *Dalstroy* trust and *Sevvostlag* (~170k-200k peak
inmates, plus Special Camp No. 5 *Berlag*) shipped ~740 000 prisoners by
steamer from Vladivostok/Nakhodka to Nagaevo Bay (Magadan, 55k in
1953AD) to build the 2 000 km Kolyma Highway and mine gold, tin and
uranium at Susuman, Yagodnoye, Butugychag and Pevek (Chaun-Chukotka), at
the cost of ~130 000 to 150 000 prisoner deaths
[\[13\]](#ref-khlevniuk2004history)[\[16\]](#ref-applebaum2003gulag).

Region 45 (Southern Far East: Amur, Jewish AO *Birobidzhan*, Khabarovsk,
Primorye & 1945 South Sakhalin/Kurils) grew more than threefold
(+256,9%) from 1 020k (1926AD) to 2 560k (1941AD) and 3 640k (1953AD),
receiving ~17k Latvian *Priboi* deportees in Amur Oblast (1949AD)
alongside *Bamlag* (260k prisoners at Svobodny), Komsomolsk-on-Amur
(0,3k -\> 71k -\> 160k), the Jewish Autonomous Oblast (Birobidzhan,
established 7 May 1934AD, 108,9k in 1939AD) and post-August 1945AD
Southern Sakhalin (*Karafuto* -\> Yuzhno-Sakhalinsk) and the Kurils,
where ~290 000 to 400 000 Japanese civilians were repatriated
(1946-1949AD, leaving ~28k-43k stateless Sakhalin Koreans) and replaced
by \>350 000 Soviet settlers
[\[4\]](#ref-polian2001against)[\[12\]](#ref-martin2001affirmative)[\[18\]](#ref-strods2002priboi).

## The Great Patriotic War (1941-1945AD), the Holocaust, Refined Leningrad Siege Zone (Region 17), and the 1946-1947AD Famine

Across the western and southern regions of our 45-region partition
(Regions 1-17, 21, 22, 27 and 29), the Great Patriotic War
(1941-1945AD), the Holocaust, post-war population exchanges
(1944-1948AD) and the 1946-1947AD famine left deep, lasting demographic
scars (Table ). Byelorussian SSR (Region 6: Western Belarus & Region 7:
Eastern BSSR): Occupied from June 1941AD to July 1944AD, Belarus lost
2,2M to 2,5M inhabitants (~25% of its population), including ~750k to
800k Jews murdered in the Minsk Ghetto (~100k), Maly Trostenets,
Brest/Bronnaya Gora (~50k), Grodno, Pinsk, Vitebsk, Mogilev and Gomel,
and 628 villages (such as Khatyn) burned with all inhabitants ([archived
PDF](papers/soviet_belarus_wwii_demography_2004_1.pdf); [archived
PDF](papers/soviet_belarus_wwii_demography_2024_2.pdf))
[\[23\]](#ref-arad2009holocaust)[\[37\]](#ref-bylov2004belarus)[\[83\]](#ref-korsak2024belarus).
Region 7 fell by -28,5% from 5 850k (1941AD) to 4 180k (1945AD, 4 890k
in 1953AD), while Region 6 dropped by -28,1% from 2 950k (1941AD) to 2
120k (1945AD, 2 310k in 1953AD) as ~274 200 ethnic Poles were
transferred to Poland under the 1944-1946AD Lublin treaties.

Ukrainian SSR (Regions 8-14): Combined population across Ukrainian
Regions 8-15 plunged by -11 410k (-27,5%) from 41 560k (1941AD) to 30
150k (1945AD), driven by ~6,8M to 8,0M war deaths (including ~1,5M to
1,6M Holocaust victims at Babyn Yar in Kyiv 33 771, Kamianets-Podilskyi
23 600, Lviv/Janowska & Belzec, Drobytsky Yar in Kharkiv, and
Romanian-occupied Odesa/Transnistria), 2,4M *Ostarbeiter* deportations
and eastern evacuation ([archived
PDF](papers/soviet_holocaust_occupied_ussr_2026_1.pdf))
[\[23\]](#ref-arad2009holocaust)[\[41\]](#ref-mesle2012mortalite)[\[84\]](#ref-kornilova2026aktsiya1005).
In Region 8 (Galicia & Volhynia: 7 740k in 1941AD -\> 5 180k in 1945AD,
-33,1%), the Holocaust (~700k murdered) was followed by the 1944-1946AD
Polish-Ukrainian population exchange (~810 000 Poles transferred from
Western Ukraine to Poland and ~482 800 Ukrainians transferred from
Poland into Ukraine, plus ~140k Ukrainians deported inside Poland during
1947AD *Operation Vistula*) and the NKVD deportation of ~203 000 Western
Ukrainians accused of aiding the OUN-UPA insurgency (culminating in
Operation *Zapad* in October 1947AD, which exiled 77 791 people in three
days to Karaganda, Omsk, Kemerovo and Vorkuta)
[\[4\]](#ref-polian2001against)[\[6\]](#ref-zemskov2003spetsposelentsy)[\[28\]](#ref-statiev2010borderlands).
In Region 9 (Northern Bukovina, Budjak & Transcarpathia: 2 780k in
1941AD -\> 1 920k in 1945AD), Transcarpathia (Zakarpattia / Uzhhorod)
was ceded by Czechoslovakia on 29 June 1945AD
[\[4\]](#ref-polian2001against).

Refined Region 17 (Leningrad Siege Metropolis, Novgorod, and Pskov
Oblasts): Separated cleanly from Kaliningrad Oblast (Region 5), Region
17 isolates the pure demographic shock of the 872-day Siege of Leningrad
(8 September 1941AD - 27 January 1944AD) and the scorched-earth
Wehrmacht occupation of Novgorod and Pskov ([archived
PDF](papers/soviet_leningrad_siege_demography_2026_1.pdf); [archived
PDF](papers/soviet_leningrad_siege_demography_2019_2.pdf))
[\[85\]](#ref-lomagin2002neizvestnaya)[\[86\]](#ref-boldovsky2019leningrad)[\[87\]](#ref-kulish2026leningrad).
Between September 1941AD and January 1944AD, 800 000 to 1 000 000
civilians starved or froze to death inside Leningrad while 1,74M
residents (including ~89k Leningrad Germans and Ingrian Finns deported
to Siberia and the Lena Delta) were evacuated across Lake Ladoga’s *Road
of Life*, shrinking the city from 3,19M (1939AD) to 546k in January
1944AD (920k by late 1945AD), while Novgorod (40k in 1939AD -\> fewer
than 3k civilians at liberation in January 1944AD, 19k in 1945AD) and
Staraya Russa were reduced to rubble. Consequently, Region 17 plunged by
-56,5% from 6 960k in June 1941AD to 3 030k in 1943AD, recovering via
re-evacuation and the 1944-1945AD border transfers of Ivangorod/Pechory
(from Estonia) and Pytalovo/Abrene (from Latvia) to 4 340k in 1945AD
(-37,6%) and 5 490k in 1953AD (still -21,1% below 1941AD, further
battered politically during the 1949-1950AD “Leningrad Affair”).

Frontline Russian Regions 21, 22, 27 and 29 and the 1946-1947AD Post-War
Famine: Along the Smolensk-Rzhev-Vyazma-Bryansk axis, Region 21 (Upper
Volga & Western Front) dropped by -25,8% (13 280k in 1941AD -\> 9 850k
in 1945AD, 10 790k in 1953AD); Region 22 (Central Black-Earth Belt:
Voronezh, Kursk, Oryol) fell by -25,3% (13 850k -\> 10 350k); and Region
27 (Saratov, Stalingrad & Astrakhan) fell by -19,3% (4 250k -\> 3 430k,
with Stalingrad reduced from 445k to 32k in February 1943AD).
Immediately after the war, the severe 1946AD drought across Moldavia,
Ukraine, the Central Black-Earth Belt, the Lower Volga and West
Siberia - compounded by 17,5M tonnes of state grain procurements, grain
exports and the September 1946AD removal of 27,5M rural inhabitants from
state bread rationing - triggered the 1946-1947AD Soviet Famine (1,0M to
1,5M total excess deaths: 115k-200k in the Moldavian SSR Region 4 \[2
080k in 1945AD -\> 1 940k in 1947AD\], ~300k-500k across Ukraine Regions
9, 10, 11 and 14 \[Region 11: 6 750k -\> 6 720k\], and ~500k-700k across
Russia Regions 21, 22, 27, 29, 35 and 38 \[Region 22: 10 350k -\> 10
250k\]) before late Stalinist demographic recovery carried the 45-region
total to 165 350k in March 1953AD ([archived
PDF](papers/soviet_famine_1946_1947_2012_1.pdf); [archived
PDF](papers/soviet_lenin_stalin_moldavia_famine_casu_2020.pdf))
[\[26\]](#ref-ellman2000famine1947)[\[27\]](#ref-casu2020famine1947)[\[67\]](#ref-zima1996golod)[\[81\]](#ref-laperdin2013omsk1947)[\[82\]](#ref-laperdin2012westsiberia1947).
